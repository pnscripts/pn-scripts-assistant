# Houseplant care

Light decides what grows; watering kills most houseplants. Match the plant to the spot, then water only when the soil says so.

## Steps
1. **Identify the plants.** Name (or photo), pot size and material, whether the pot has drainage holes, where it stands (window direction, distance from the window), room temperature, heating or air-conditioning nearby, and pets or small children at home.
2. **Assess the light.**
   - Bright direct: in or right next to a sunny window (south-facing in the northern hemisphere).
   - Bright indirect: near a bright window but out of direct midday sun.
   - Medium: a few metres from a window.
   - Low: far from windows or a north-facing room; few plants thrive, many only survive.
3. **Match plant to light.**
   - Direct sun: cacti, most succulents, citrus, some herbs.
   - Bright indirect: monstera, fiddle-leaf fig, rubber plant, many palms and orchids.
   - Medium to low: snake plant, ZZ plant, pothos, philodendron (heart-leaf), cast-iron plant.
4. **Set watering by soil check.** Push a finger in: succulents and cacti when fully dry; most foliage plants when the top 2–5 cm are dry; ferns and calatheas when the surface just starts to dry. Water thoroughly until it drains, then empty the saucer.
5. **Pot and soil.** Always use drainage holes; decorative pots without holes should hold a plastic inner pot. Use a mix suited to the plant (gritty for succulents, chunky for orchids and aroids).
6. **Humidity and temperature.** Keep away from radiators and cold draughts; group humidity-loving plants or use a tray of pebbles and water.
7. **Feeding.** Feed during active growth (usually spring and summer) at the label dose or weaker; little or none in winter.
8. **Repotting.** When roots circle the pot or grow out of the holes, usually every 1–3 years in spring, into a pot only 2–5 cm wider.
9. **Seasonal shifts.** Less water in winter, more light needed; rotate plants a quarter turn regularly for even growth.

## Care card template
```
Plant: Pothos | Light: medium to bright indirect | Pet-safe: NO (toxic to cats/dogs)
Water: when top 3 cm dry (about weekly in summer, every 2-3 weeks in winter)
Feed: monthly, spring-summer, half strength | Repot: spring, when root-bound
Signs: yellow lower leaves = too wet; pale, long stems = too little light
```

## Pet and child safety
Many common houseplants are toxic if chewed (for example pothos, philodendron, dieffenbachia, peace lily, aloe, and lilies which are very dangerous for cats). Tell the user to check each plant with a reliable poison database or a vet, and to place risky plants out of reach. If a pet or child has eaten a plant and shows symptoms, contact a vet or poison centre or the emergency number (112 in the EU) right away.

## Pitfalls
- Watering on a fixed schedule regardless of the season and soil.
- Pots without drainage; roots rot.
- Moving a struggling plant around every few days; plants need time to adjust.
- Repotting into a much larger pot; the extra wet soil causes rot.
- Fertilising a sick or dry plant; fix the cause first.
