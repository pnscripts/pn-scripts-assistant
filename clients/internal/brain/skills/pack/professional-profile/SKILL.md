# Professional profile

A profile should tell the right reader in 10 seconds what the user does, for whom, and why they are credible - and make it easy to get in touch.

## Steps
1. **Define the target reader:** recruiters for a specific role, clients for a service, partners, conference organisers. Ask for the user's CV, current profile text and 2-3 example profiles they admire.
2. **Headline** (the most visible line): role or specialism + who you help + proof or focus. Use words the target reader searches for.
   - Weak: "Looking for new opportunities"
   - Better: "Accountant for small e-commerce businesses | VAT, bookkeeping, monthly reporting"
3. **Summary / About** (about 150-300 words, first person):
   - Line 1-2: what you do and for whom (shown before "see more").
   - Proof: 2-3 results or notable clients/projects.
   - How you work or what makes you different.
   - Call to action: how to contact or what you are open to.
4. **Experience:** 2-4 achievement bullets per recent role (same style as the CV), plus media links to work samples.
5. **Skills and keywords:** the 5-10 most relevant, matching job ads or client searches; ask colleagues for recommendations that mention specific results.
6. **Photo and banner:** recent, clear face, neutral background, good light; banner that shows the field or offer.
7. **Short bio versions:** 1 line (for social), 50 words (for events), 100 words (for websites), third person where expected.
8. **Privacy and settings:** check what is public, open-to-work visibility, contact details (a dedicated email such as name@example.com rather than a private phone), and whether the current employer can see job-search signals. Menus change; tell the user to check the network's current settings page.
9. **Keep it consistent** with the CV: same dates, titles and facts.

## Example summary opening
"I help small online shops keep their books clean and their VAT filings on time, so owners can focus on selling. Over 8 years I have worked with 60+ shops, cut month-end closing from 10 to 3 days for most of them, and..."

## Bio examples
- 1 line: "Accountant for small online shops - VAT, bookkeeping, monthly reports."
- 50 words: "[Name] helps small e-commerce businesses keep clean books and file VAT on time. With 8 years in accounting and 60+ online shops served, she focuses on simple monthly reporting that owners actually read. She speaks regularly at local business meetups."

## Checklist
- Headline uses words the target reader searches for.
- The first two lines of the summary work on their own.
- Contact path is clear and does not expose private data.
- Facts match the CV exactly.

## Pitfalls
- Buzzwords ("passionate, results-driven guru") instead of specifics.
- A headline that is only a job title at the current company.
- Mismatch with the CV, which raises doubts.
- Oversharing personal data or confidential employer details.

## Output
A new headline (3 options), the summary, rewritten experience bullets, a skills list, bio versions (1 line, 50 and 100 words), and a short privacy and settings checklist.
