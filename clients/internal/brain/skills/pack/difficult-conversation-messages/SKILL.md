# Difficult conversation messages

Be clear about the issue, kind to the person, and specific about what happens next. The aim is a workable outcome, not winning the argument.

## Steps
1. **Cool down first.** If the user is upset, let them vent to you, then draft. Suggest sending after a pause, never in the heat of the moment.
2. **Clarify the goal.** What outcome does the user want: a change in behaviour, a refund, a decision, a repaired relationship, simply being heard? One main goal per message.
3. **Separate facts from interpretations.** List what happened (observable, dated) apart from feelings and assumptions about intent.
4. **Pick the channel.** Serious, emotional or ambiguous topics are often better as a call or meeting, with a short written summary afterwards. Use writing when a record is needed.
5. **Structure the message (one of these):**
   - *Feedback / complaint* - situation, behaviour, impact, request (SBI + ask).
   - *Saying no* - thanks, clear no, short reason, alternative if any.
   - *Apology* - what went wrong, own it without excuses, impact acknowledged, fix and prevention.
   - *Bad news* - the news early and plainly, reason, what it means for them, support or next step.
   - *Boundary* - what you will and will not do, from now on, without justifying at length.
6. **Use de-escalating language.** "I" statements, specific behaviour not character ("the report arrived after the deadline twice" not "you are unreliable"), acknowledge their view ("I understand this is frustrating").
7. **End with a concrete next step**: a question, a proposed time to talk, a deadline, or what you will do.
8. **Review for traps** (see Pitfalls) and offer a softer and a firmer version if the user is unsure.

## Templates
**Declining**
"Thanks for asking me to lead the event. I can't take it on this quarter because of the audit deadline. I'd be glad to review the plan once it's drafted."
**Critical feedback**
"In the last two client calls (3 and 10 June) the demo started 15 minutes late. Clients mentioned it, and it shortened the Q&A. Can we agree that set-up starts 30 minutes before? What would help you make that work?"
**Apology**
"I'm sorry - I sent the wrong price list on Monday and that caused confusion with your team. That was my mistake. The correct list is attached, and from now on a second person checks every list before it goes out."

## Pitfalls
- "Sandwich" feedback where the criticism gets lost between compliments.
- Apologies with "but" or "if you were offended", which cancel the apology.
- Sarcasm, absolutes ("always", "never"), CC-ing a manager as a threat.
- Over-explaining a refusal, which invites negotiation.
- Putting confidential or legally sensitive admissions in writing without thought; for disputes involving contracts, dismissal or harassment, suggest HR or legal advice.

## Output
Return the message ready to send (and a short version for chat if useful), a one-line note on the approach chosen, and two or three likely reactions with suggested responses. If a live conversation is better, give an opening line and key points instead.
