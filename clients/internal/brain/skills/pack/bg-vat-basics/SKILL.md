# Bulgarian VAT basics

Explain VAT concepts clearly and neutrally. Thresholds, rates and procedures change and have exceptions: never state a current figure as certain; tell the user to check nra.bg and to consult an accountant before registering, deregistering or filing.

## Core ideas to explain
- **Output VAT** is charged on your sales; **input VAT** is paid on your business purchases. A registered business pays the difference (output minus deductible input) or carries forward an excess.
- **Rates.** There is a standard rate (long-standing 20%), a reduced rate for listed goods and services, and 0% for some exports and intra-EU supplies. Check the current list of reduced-rate items on nra.bg.
- **Mandatory registration** applies when taxable turnover in Bulgaria exceeds the statutory threshold in a calendar year (the current or the previous one, since 2026; before 2026 it was counted over 12 consecutive months), and in some special cases regardless of turnover (e.g. certain EU acquisitions or receiving services from abroad). Check the current threshold and period on nra.bg.
- **Voluntary registration** is possible below the threshold; it allows deducting input VAT but adds monthly filing work.

## Steps
1. **Collect facts.** Legal form, business activity, turnover per month for this and the previous calendar year, who the customers are (BG business, BG consumers, EU business, EU consumers, non-EU), what is sold (goods, services, digital services), and big purchases planned.
2. **Check registration status.** Sum taxable turnover month by month and compare with the threshold the user finds on nra.bg. Flag that the deadline to apply is short once the threshold is reached.
3. **Map each customer type to its treatment** (orientation only):
   - BG customer: charge Bulgarian VAT.
   - EU business with a valid VAT number (services): usually reverse charge, invoice without VAT with the legal basis, plus a VIES declaration.
   - EU consumers (digital services, distance sales): above the EU-wide threshold, VAT of the customer's country, often through the OSS scheme.
   - Outside the EU: often outside the scope of Bulgarian VAT or 0%; depends on the service.
4. **Explain the monthly cycle.** Purchase and sales ledgers, VAT return and VIES declaration filed electronically for each month, by a fixed day of the next month (check the current day on nra.bg), with payment by the same deadline.
5. **Check input VAT deduction.** Only with a valid invoice in the company's name, for business use, and within the permitted period. Cars, entertainment and mixed use have special limits.
6. **List open questions for the accountant.**

## Example answer shape
> Your turnover this calendar year is about X so far. Compare it with the current threshold on nra.bg; if you are close, register before you cross it, because the deadline is short. Your German client (a business) is usually billed without VAT under reverse charge, which also requires a VIES declaration. Please confirm with an accountant before your next invoice.

## Pitfalls
- Counting only paid invoices: turnover usually means taxable supplies, not cash received.
- Forgetting that receiving services from abroad (ads, software) can trigger registration.
- Charging VAT before registration takes effect, or not charging it after.
- Assuming a foreign platform handles VAT for you without reading its terms.
- Quoting a threshold or rate from memory; always point to the current official figure.

## Questions for the accountant
- Based on my turnover by month, when exactly must I apply for registration?
- Which of my sales are taxable, exempt or outside the scope?
- Do my purchases of foreign services (ads, software, hosting) affect registration?
- Should I use OSS for my sales to EU consumers?
- Which input VAT can I deduct, and from what date?
- Who prepares and files the monthly return and VIES declaration, and by which day?

## Output
Hand back: a plain summary of the user's likely VAT position, the treatment per customer type, the monthly obligations, items to verify on nra.bg, and questions to take to an accountant. Say clearly it is general orientation, not tax advice.
