# Cover letter

A good cover letter is a short bridge between the job ad and the CV: it shows you read the ad, understand the company and have proof you can do the job.

## Steps
1. **Collect:** job ad, CV, company name and anything known about it (product, news, values), the hiring manager's name if known, how the application is sent (portal, email), language required.
2. **Pick the 2-3 strongest matches** between the ad's top requirements and the user's experience. Each needs a concrete example with a result.
3. **Find a genuine reason for this company:** a product the user uses, a project, a value or market position. Avoid generic praise.
4. **Structure (about 250-400 words, under one page):**
   - Opening: the role, and one strong sentence on why you fit.
   - Body 1-2: matching proofs as short stories (situation, action, result).
   - Why them: one short paragraph.
   - Close: availability, interest in a conversation, thanks.
5. **Match the tone** to the company and country: more formal for public sector, banks and law; warmer for startups and creative fields.
6. **For an email application:** clear subject ("Application - Junior Accountant - Name Surname"), the letter in the body or a short note plus the letter attached, as the ad requests.
7. **Proofread:** company and role names correct, no leftover text from another application.

## Template
```
Dear [Name / Hiring Team],

I am applying for the [role] position at [company]. With [X years] in [field] and a track record of [key result], I can help your team [goal from the ad].

At [company/role], I [action] which [result with number]. This matches your need for [requirement].
I also [second proof] - relevant to [second requirement].

I am drawn to [company] because [specific reason].

I would welcome the chance to discuss how I can contribute. I am available from [date] and can be reached at [phone] or name@example.com.

Kind regards,
[Name]
```

## Example opening
Weak: "I am a highly motivated individual seeking new challenges."
Better: "I am applying for the Customer Success Manager role because I have spent four years keeping B2B software customers happy - last year my accounts renewed at 96%."

## Pitfalls
- Repeating the CV line by line.
- Talking only about what the user wants, not what the employer gets.
- One letter reused for every company; recruiters notice.
- Overlong letters; a busy reader skims the first lines.

## Output
The finished letter (and an email subject line if relevant), a note of which requirements it covers, and any facts the user should confirm or add.
