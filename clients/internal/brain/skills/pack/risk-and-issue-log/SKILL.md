# Risk and issue log

A risk is something that might happen; an issue is something that has happened. Log both, give each an owner and a next action, and review them weekly.

## Steps
1. **Identify risks** with the team using prompts: people (availability, skills, key-person dependency), suppliers and third parties, technology, money, legal and compliance, schedule, scope changes, external events (weather, holidays, regulations).
2. **Write each risk as cause -> event -> effect:** "Because the supplier has one machine, a breakdown could delay delivery by two weeks, pushing the launch past the holiday season."
3. **Score** likelihood (1-5) and impact (1-5); score = L x I. 15+ high, 8-14 medium, below 8 low. Agree the scale with the team so scores mean the same to everyone.
4. **Choose a response:**
   - Avoid - change the plan so the risk cannot happen.
   - Reduce - lower likelihood or impact (second supplier, earlier test).
   - Transfer - insurance, contract terms, outsourcing.
   - Accept - with a contingency plan and budget if it happens.
5. **Set an owner, a trigger and a review date** for each risk. The trigger is the early sign that the risk is turning into an issue.
6. **Log issues** separately: what happened, impact, priority, owner, action, due date, status. Link the issue to the risk it came from, if any.
7. **Escalate** when an issue threatens a milestone, budget or quality and the owner cannot solve it within their authority. Say what decision is needed and by when.
8. **Review weekly:** close old risks, rescore, add new ones, report the top 3 in the status update.

## Template
| ID | Type | Description (cause -> event -> effect) | L | I | Score | Response | Owner | Trigger | Next action / due | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Risk | Single supplier -> machine failure -> 2-week delay | 2 | 5 | 10 | Reduce: quote from second supplier | Ana | Supplier misses weekly update | Get quote by 12 May | Open |
| I1 | Issue | Venue double-booked for 3 June | - | 4 | High | Find alternative venue | Ivo | - | Shortlist 3 venues by Friday | In progress |

## Example escalation message
"Issue I1 (venue double-booked for 3 June) threatens the launch event. We have three alternatives at +10-20% cost. Decision needed from the sponsor by Friday: approve the extra cost or move the event to 10 June. If no decision, we lose the remaining venue options."

## Questions to find hidden risks
- What did go wrong on the last similar project?
- Which single person or supplier could stop us?
- What are we assuming that nobody has checked?

## Pitfalls
- Vague risks ("budget", "time") that nobody can act on.
- Logging risks once and never reviewing them.
- Hiding issues until they become crises; early escalation is a strength.
- Owners without authority or time to act.

## Output
The filled risk and issue table, the top 3 risks with responses, any issue needing escalation with the decision required, and the next review date.
