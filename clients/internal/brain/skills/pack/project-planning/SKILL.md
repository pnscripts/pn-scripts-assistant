# Project planning

A plan is a shared answer to: why, what exactly, by when, by whom, with what, and how we will know it worked. Keep it to one page that people actually read.

## Steps
1. **Clarify the why.** Ask what problem the project solves and what changes when it is done. Write one goal sentence.
2. **Make the goal measurable.** Success criteria with numbers and dates, e.g. "new site live by 15 March with all 40 products and checkout tested".
3. **Fix the scope.** List what is in and, just as important, what is out. Out-of-scope items prevent silent growth of the work.
4. **List deliverables** - the tangible things handed over (site, report, trained staff, event).
5. **Set milestones.** 4-8 checkpoints with dates working backwards from the deadline; each ends with something that can be shown or checked.
6. **Assign roles.** One accountable owner per deliverable; name the decision-maker and who must be consulted or informed.
7. **Map dependencies and constraints:** external suppliers, approvals, holidays, budget limits, people's availability.
8. **Budget.** Main cost lines plus a contingency (often 10-20% for familiar work, more for new kinds of work).
9. **Top risks.** 3-5 risks with an owner and a response (see the risk log skill for more).
10. **Communication rhythm.** Weekly status, where documents live, how decisions are recorded.

## One-page template
```
Project: [name]            Owner: [name]         Sponsor/decision-maker: [name]
Goal: [one sentence]
Success criteria: [measurable, dated]
In scope: [...]            Out of scope: [...]
Deliverables: [...]
Milestones: [date - checkpoint] x 4-8
Team and roles: [who does / decides / is consulted / is informed]
Budget: [total + contingency]
Dependencies and constraints: [...]
Top risks and responses: [...]
Communication: [weekly status day, channel, document location]
```

## Example
Goal: "Move the office to the new building over one weekend with no more than half a day of downtime." Out of scope: new furniture purchase. Milestones: lease signed, IT survey done, movers booked, packing complete, move weekend, snag list closed.

## Questions to ask when information is missing
- Who will say "this is done" and accept the result?
- What happens if the deadline slips by two weeks?
- Which is fixed - the date, the budget or the scope? Usually only one can be fully fixed.

## Pitfalls
- Starting with tasks before agreeing on the goal and scope.
- Milestones without a checkable output ("design phase 50%").
- Several owners for one deliverable means nobody owns it.
- A plan written once and never updated; review it at every status meeting.

## Output
The completed one-page plan, a list of open questions and assumptions, and the first three actions for this week with owners.
