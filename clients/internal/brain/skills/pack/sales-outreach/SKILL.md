# Sales outreach

Outreach works when it is about the prospect's problem, short, and asks for one small step.

## Steps
1. **Define the offer and the reader.** What exactly do we sell, to which role in which kind of company, and which problem does it solve? Ask for proof points: results, clients who agreed to be named, numbers the user can back up.
2. **Research the prospect.** Use only what is public and relevant: their website, recent news, job ads, product launches. Find one specific trigger (new branch, hiring, a visible problem).
3. **Write the first message** with this structure:
   - Subject: 3-6 words, specific, no clickbait ("Faster invoicing for Acme's new branch").
   - Line 1: the trigger, about them, not us.
   - Lines 2-3: the problem and what we do about it, with one proof point.
   - Line 4: one low-effort ask (a 15-minute call, a yes/no question, permission to send a sample).
   - Signature with name, company, website and an easy way to opt out.
4. **Keep it short.** 60-120 words for email, under 300 characters for a connection note.
5. **Plan follow-ups.** 2-3 follow-ups, 3-7 days apart, each adding something new (a case, a short tip, a question). Stop after the last one or on any "no".
6. **Check compliance.** Business contacts only, a legitimate interest that fits their role, sender clearly identified, opt-out honoured at once. In the EU this falls under GDPR and e-privacy rules; tell the user to check local rules for unsolicited email before a campaign.
7. **Personalise at scale carefully.** Use merge fields only for facts you verified; a wrong name or company is worse than no personalisation.

## Template
Subject: Quicker month-end for {Company}

Hi {First name},

I saw that {Company} opened a second office in {City} this spring. Teams growing that way often find month-end reporting takes twice as long.

We help finance teams of 10-50 people cut that time by automating the bank-to-ledger matching. {Reference client} went from 5 days to 2.

Would a 15-minute call next week be useful? If not relevant, just say so and I will not write again.

{Name}, {Company} - name@example.com

## Follow-up example
"Hi {First name}, one more idea: most of the time saved comes from the bank feed alone, which takes an afternoon to set up. Happy to show it in 15 minutes. If timing is wrong, no problem."

## Pitfalls
- Opening with "I hope this finds you well" or a paragraph about our company.
- Several asks or links in one message; it lowers replies and looks like spam.
- Invented or unverifiable claims ("the best in Europe"); use real, checkable results.
- Fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subjects and false urgency; they damage trust and may break rules.
- Bought lists and sending to personal addresses without a lawful basis.
- Ignoring an opt-out or replying to "no" with another pitch.

## Output
Return the first message (subject and body), the follow-up sequence with timing, the personalisation fields to fill, and a short compliance note on what to check before sending. Never send anything on the user's behalf without their explicit approval.
