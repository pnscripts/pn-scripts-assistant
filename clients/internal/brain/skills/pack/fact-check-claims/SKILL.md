# Fact-check claims

Check claims one at a time against the best available source. Report what the evidence shows, including when it is unclear. Never state a verdict you could not support with a source.

## Steps
1. **Extract checkable claims.** Split the text into single factual statements. Skip opinions and predictions, or mark them "not checkable". Keep the exact wording.
2. **Prioritise.** Check first the claims that carry the argument, contain numbers, or could cause harm if wrong.
3. **Find the origin.** Search for the exact phrase or number. Trace it back to the first publication: official statistics, the study, the full speech or transcript, the law text, the company filing.
4. **Compare carefully.** Same figure, unit, period, country and definition? Is a quote complete or cut so that its meaning changes? Is a photo or video from the stated time and place (look for earlier copies with a reverse image search)?
5. **Seek independent confirmation** for anything important: at least two independent sources, or one authoritative primary source.
6. **Give the verdict** using the scale below and cite the sources with dates.
7. **Suggest a correction** in neutral wording when a claim is wrong or misleading.

## Verdict scale
- **True** - matches primary evidence.
- **Mostly true** - right in substance, small error in number, date or wording.
- **Misleading** - technically accurate but missing context that changes the meaning.
- **False** - contradicted by reliable evidence.
- **Unverified** - no reliable evidence either way yet.

## Checklist for numbers
- [ ] Same unit and base (percent vs percentage points, per capita vs total)
- [ ] Same time period and geography
- [ ] Nominal vs inflation-adjusted
- [ ] Correlation not presented as cause
- [ ] Sample size and margin of error known for surveys

## Template
```
Claim: "<exact words>"
Verdict: <True / Mostly true / Misleading / False / Unverified>
Evidence: <what the primary source says, with figure>
Source: <publisher, title, date, link or reference>
Note: <context or suggested correct wording>
```

## Example
Claim: "Unemployment fell by 50% last year." Source data: rate went from 6% to 5%.
Verdict: Misleading - the rate fell by 1 percentage point (about 17%), not by half.

## Pitfalls
- Many articles repeating a claim may all trace back to one unverified source.
- Screenshots of posts or headlines are easy to fake; find the original post.
- Old stories recirculate as new; check the date.
- Satire sites are often shared as news.
- Do not rely on your own memory for current figures; look them up and give the date.

## Output
Give a table or list with each claim, verdict, the key evidence and source, then a short overall assessment and suggested corrections.
