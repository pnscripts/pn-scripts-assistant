# Executive brief

A decision-maker reads the first lines and decides whether to read on. Lead with the conclusion and the ask. Everything else supports it. One page, plain words, real numbers.

## Steps
1. **Name the reader and the decision.** Who reads it, what must they decide or know, and by when? If no decision is needed, say "for information".
2. **Write the bottom line first** in 1-2 sentences: the situation and what you recommend.
3. **State the ask** precisely: approve, choose, fund, note. Include amount, date and owner.
4. **Give context** in 2-3 sentences: why now, what happens if nothing is done.
5. **Lay out options** (two or three, including "do nothing" when relevant) with cost, benefit, time and main risk each.
6. **Recommend** one option and give the deciding reason.
7. **List risks and mitigations** - the top three only.
8. **Add next steps** with owners and dates.
9. **Cut.** Remove jargon, history and detail the reader does not need; move evidence to an appendix or link.

## Template
```
Subject: <decision needed> - by <date>

Bottom line: <recommendation in 1-2 sentences>
Ask: <approve / choose / fund> <what, how much, by when>

Context: ...

Options
| Option | Cost | Benefit | Time | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| B | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Do nothing | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Recommendation: Option <X>, because ...
Risks and mitigations: ...
Next steps: <who, what, when>
```

## Example
Bottom line: "Our support backlog doubled in three months; I recommend hiring one temporary agent for six months rather than buying new software now." Ask: approve the budget for one temporary role from 1 June. Options table compares hire, software and do nothing, with response times and costs.

## Checklist
- [ ] Recommendation and ask fit in the first three lines
- [ ] Numbers are specific and sourced, with units and dates
- [ ] Each option has a cost and a risk
- [ ] No unexplained abbreviations
- [ ] Fits on one page (about 250-400 words)

## Pitfalls
- Building up to the conclusion like a story; leaders may never reach it.
- Presenting only the favoured option; credible briefs show alternatives.
- Vague asks ("support this initiative") instead of a concrete decision.
- Hiding bad news; state it plainly with the plan to handle it.

## Output
Hand back the one-page brief in the template, plus a list of any figures or facts the user must confirm before sending.
