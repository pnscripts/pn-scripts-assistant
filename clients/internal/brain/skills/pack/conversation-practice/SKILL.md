# Conversation practice

The aim is for the learner to produce as much language as possible, comfortably, with just enough correction to improve.

## Steps
1. **Set up the session.** Ask: language, level (A1–C2), topic or situation, length (for example 10 exchanges), and correction style: "correct every mistake", "only important ones", or "only at the end".
2. **Agree on the scenario.** Give a one-line setting and roles: "You are at a pharmacy; I am the pharmacist." Offer 3 options if the user has no idea.
3. **Match the level.**
   - A1–A2: short sentences, common words, one question at a time, offer two answer choices if the learner is stuck.
   - B1–B2: natural pace, follow-up questions, some idioms with a gloss.
   - C1–C2: native-like speech, opinions, disagreement, humour.
4. **Keep the learner talking.** Ask open questions, react to content (not only form), and keep your turns shorter than theirs.
5. **Correct gently.** Use recasts: repeat the learner's idea correctly inside your reply. For important errors add a short note in brackets after your turn, in the agreed style.
6. **Help when stuck.** If the learner writes in their native language or says "how do I say…", give the phrase, then let them use it.
7. **Close with feedback.** List 3 things done well, the 3–5 most useful corrections, 5 new phrases to keep, and one suggestion for next time.

## Example (Spanish, A2, café, corrections only important)
```
Assistant: ¡Hola! ¿Qué quieres tomar?
Learner:   Yo quiero un café con leche y un croissant, por favor.
Assistant: Muy bien, un café con leche y un cruasán. ¿Para tomar aquí o para llevar?
Learner:   Para aquí. ¿Cuánto es costa?
Assistant: Son cuatro euros con cincuenta. [Note: "¿Cuánto cuesta?" or "¿Cuánto es?" - not both]
```

## Useful scenarios
Ordering food, asking for directions, checking in at a hotel, a doctor's appointment, a job interview, small talk with a neighbour, complaining about a faulty product, phoning to book an appointment, describing your weekend, debating a news topic.

## Pitfalls
- Long assistant turns that leave the learner nothing to do.
- Correcting every small slip mid-flow when the user asked for fluency practice.
- Switching to the native language too early; try a simpler rephrase first.
- Repeating the same scenario; vary topics so new vocabulary is needed.
- Losing track of the agreed level; if the learner keeps answering in single words, slow down and simplify.
- Unnatural textbook phrases; prefer what people actually say, and note register (formal/informal "you").
- Pretending to hear pronunciation in text chat; for pronunciation suggest recording and comparing with native audio.

## Output
During the session: in-character replies at the agreed level with corrections in the agreed style. At the end: a short feedback block (strengths, key corrections, new phrases, next-step suggestion).
