# Citation and attribution

Every borrowed fact, number, quote, image or idea needs a visible source. Pick one citation style and apply it consistently. If the user's school, journal or publisher has a style guide, follow that guide over these notes.

## Steps
1. **Collect the details** for each source: author(s), year and full date if relevant, title, container (journal, website, book), volume and issue, pages, publisher, DOI or URL, date accessed for pages that change.
2. **Choose the style.** Ask which style is required; if none, use APA for sciences and business, MLA for humanities, Chicago notes for history, or a simple "Author, Title, Publisher, Year, link" for blogs and internal documents.
3. **Cite in the text** at the point of use, not only at the end.
4. **Build the reference list** with every in-text source and nothing else, ordered as the style requires.
5. **Handle quotes.** Use quotation marks for exact words, keep them short, mark omissions with [...] and changes with [square brackets], and give the page number.
6. **Handle paraphrase.** Rewrite in your own structure and words and still cite. Changing a few words is not paraphrase.
7. **Handle images and data.** Check the licence (public domain, Creative Commons type, or permission needed). Credit as the licence requires, e.g. "Photo: Author Name, CC BY 4.0".
8. **Final check** with the checklist.

## Style examples (journal article)
- APA: `Ivanova, M., & Smith, J. (2023). Remote work and productivity. Journal of Work Studies, 12(3), 45-60. https://doi.org/10.0000/example`
- MLA: `Ivanova, Maria, and John Smith. "Remote Work and Productivity." Journal of Work Studies, vol. 12, no. 3, 2023, pp. 45-60.`
- Harvard in text: `(Ivanova and Smith, 2023, p. 47)`
- Web page, simple style: `Organisation Name, "Page title", updated 5 March 2024, link, accessed 2 April 2024.`

## Checklist
- [ ] Every quote, number and non-obvious fact has a citation
- [ ] In-text citations match the reference list one to one
- [ ] One style used throughout, including punctuation and capitals
- [ ] DOIs used where they exist
- [ ] Images and data have a licence and credit line
- [ ] Secondary citations marked "as cited in" when the original was not read

## Pitfalls
- Citing a source you have not seen; cite the one you actually read.
- AI tools and reference generators can produce fake or wrong references; verify each one exists.
- "Found on Google" or "from the internet" is not a source.
- Common knowledge (e.g. the capital of a country) needs no citation; specific figures do.
- Creative Commons "NC" or "ND" licences may forbid commercial use or edits.
- Translating a text yourself does not remove the need to cite the original.
- Listing a source in the bibliography is not enough if a passage is copied without quotation marks.

## Output
Return the corrected in-text citations, the formatted reference list in the chosen style, and a short list of missing details or licence questions to resolve.
