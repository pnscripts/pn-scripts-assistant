# Customer support replies

The customer wants three things: to be understood, a solution, and to know what happens next.

## Steps
1. **Read the whole message and history.** Identify the real request (refund, fix, information, apology), the order or account details, what was already promised, and the customer's emotion.
2. **Check facts and policy.** Ask the user for what you cannot know: order status, the return or refund policy, warranty, what the company is willing to offer. Never invent a policy, a date or compensation.
3. **Choose the outcome.** Solve now, give a workaround, escalate, or say no with a reason and an alternative.
4. **Write the reply** with this structure:
   - Greeting with the customer's name.
   - Acknowledge the specific problem in one sentence (not "we apologise for any inconvenience").
   - Apologise once if the company was at fault; own it without blaming others.
   - The answer or solution, in numbered steps if there are actions.
   - What happens next and when (a date or time frame you can keep).
   - Close with a way to reply and the agent's name.
5. **Match the channel.** Chat: 1-3 short lines per message. Email: 80-180 words. Public review reply: short, polite, no personal or order data, invite them to a private channel.
6. **Check before sending** (checklist below).

## Checklist
- Every question in the customer's message is answered.
- No personal data in public replies; no full card numbers or passwords ever requested.
- Promises are ones the company can keep; dates are real.
- Tone is calm and human, no sarcasm, no blame, no jargon.
- Legal rights (e.g. statutory return or warranty rights for consumers) are not denied; if unsure, tell the user to check the current consumer protection rules.
- One clear next step for the customer.

## Example
Customer: "Third time I write! My order #4821 still hasn't arrived and nobody answers."

Reply:
"Hello Maria,

I am sorry - you should not have to write three times, and your order #4821 is late. I checked: it left our warehouse on 20 October and the courier marked it as delayed at the local depot.

I have asked the courier to prioritise it, and you should receive it by Wednesday, 29 October. If it has not arrived by then, reply to this email and I will send a replacement or a full refund, whichever you prefer.

Kind regards,
Alex, Customer Support"

## Saying no well
"I understand why you'd like a refund after 60 days. Our return period is 30 days, so I can't refund this order, but I can offer a free repair under warranty. Shall I arrange the pickup?"

## Pitfalls
- Copy-paste macros that ignore what the customer actually wrote.
- Over-apologising in every sentence; once, sincerely, is enough.
- Asking the customer for information they already gave.
- Arguing about who is right in a public review.
- Closing the ticket before the customer confirms the fix.

## Output
Return the ready-to-send reply, plus a short internal note: facts you assumed and must be checked, any escalation needed, and a suggested macro version if the issue repeats. Do not send it yourself unless the user explicitly approves.
