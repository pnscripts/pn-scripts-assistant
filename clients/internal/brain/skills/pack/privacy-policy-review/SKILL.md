# Privacy policy review

Read the policy and translate it into plain language. Work only from the text the user provides or a page they confirm is the official one; never open a link before checking it is the service's real domain (see `link-verification`). This is not legal advice; for a policy the user publishes for a business, recommend a review by a lawyer.

## Steps
1. **Confirm the mode.** Is the user a person deciding whether to use a service, or an owner checking their own notice? The output differs.
2. **Extract the facts** into a table: who the controller is and how to contact them; what data is collected (provided, observed, inferred, from third parties); purposes; legal bases; recipients and processors; international transfers; retention; user rights; cookies and tracking; children; changes to the policy; date of the last update.
3. **Score against the checklist** below. Mark each item present, vague or missing.
4. **Highlight red flags** with the exact sentence quoted (short quotes only).
5. **For a person:** summarise what matters for them and the privacy settings or opt-outs they can use right away.
6. **For an owner:** list missing or unclear sections and propose plain wording; check the notice matches what the site really does (analytics, forms, newsletter, payment provider).

## Checklist
- [ ] Controller identity and contact (and data-protection officer if any)
- [ ] Specific purposes, each with a legal basis
- [ ] Categories of data and sources
- [ ] Named categories of recipients and processors
- [ ] Transfers outside the EU/EEA and the safeguard used
- [ ] Concrete retention periods or criteria
- [ ] Rights listed with how to exercise them and the right to complain to the authority
- [ ] Cookies and trackers explained, with a real choice
- [ ] Plain language, dated, versioned

## Red flags
- "We may share your data with partners" with no list or purpose.
- Selling or "monetising" data, data brokers, cross-site tracking for ads by default.
- Retention "as long as necessary" with no criteria.
- Rights only by post, or a fee to exercise them.
- Collecting contacts, precise location or microphone data without a clear need.
- Policy differs between languages or contradicts the app's permission requests.

## Example finding
> Section 5 says "we keep your data as long as we deem necessary". This is vague; ask for a period or criteria (e.g. "24 months after your last login"). Until then, delete your account when you stop using the service.

## Pitfalls
- Judging a policy by its length; long can still be vague.
- Ignoring the cookie banner and app permissions, which often reveal more.
- Copying another company's policy for your own site: it will describe the wrong processing.

## Output
Hand back: a short plain-language summary, the filled fact table, the checklist score, quoted red flags, concrete actions (settings to change, questions to ask, wording to add), and the note that this is not legal advice, with a recommendation to have a business policy checked by a lawyer.
