# Plain-language writing

Plain language means the reader can find what they need, understand it and act on it. It is not dumbing down; precision stays, fog goes.

## Steps
1. **Identify the reader and their task.** Who reads this, what do they already know, what must they do after reading (pay, sign, choose, call, nothing)?
2. **Lead with what matters to them.** Put the action, deadline or answer at the top. Background comes later or not at all.
3. **Use everyday words.** Replace jargon and abstract nouns:
   - "utilise" -> "use", "commence" -> "start", "in the event that" -> "if"
   - "make a payment" -> "pay", "carry out an assessment" -> "assess"
   Keep a necessary technical term but explain it once, the first time.
4. **Write short, active sentences.** Aim for an average of 15-20 words. Say who does what: "We will send your card" rather than "Your card will be dispatched".
5. **Talk to the reader.** Use "you" and "we". Use "must" for obligations, not "shall".
6. **Structure for scanning.** Descriptive headings, numbered steps for procedures, bullets for options, a table for comparisons, bold only for the few key items such as deadlines.
7. **Keep it accurate.** Check every simplified sentence against the original. If a legal or medical meaning could change, keep the exact wording and add a plain explanation next to it, and flag it for the author.
8. **Test it.** Could someone answer "what do I need to do, by when, and what happens if I don't?" after one read? Read it aloud; trip points need fixing.

## Checklist
- [ ] Main action and deadline in the first paragraph.
- [ ] No unexplained abbreviations or jargon.
- [ ] Mostly active voice, short sentences.
- [ ] Numbers, dates and amounts exact and easy to spot.
- [ ] Contact or next step clear at the end.
- [ ] Meaning checked line by line against the original.

## Example
Original: "Pursuant to the terms of the agreement, failure to remit the outstanding balance within 14 days of the date of this notice shall result in the suspension of services."
Plain: "Please pay the amount you owe within 14 days of the date on this letter. If you do not, we will stop your service. To pay or ask a question, contact billing@example.com."

## Pitfalls
- Losing a legally important condition while simplifying.
- Being patronising: short does not mean childish.
- Replacing one jargon with another ("leverage", "synergies").
- Hiding the bad news in the middle; say it clearly and kindly.
- Relying only on readability scores; they do not measure meaning or order.

## Output
Return the plain-language version, then a short list of terms you replaced or explained and any sentence where exact meaning needs the author's or a specialist's confirmation.
