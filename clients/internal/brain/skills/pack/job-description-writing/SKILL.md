# Job description writing

A good job description tells the right person "this is for me" and the wrong person "this is not", in under two minutes of reading.

## Steps
1. **Collect the facts.** Ask for: job title, team, reporting line, location and remote policy, contract type and hours, start date, pay range, the 3-5 outcomes expected in the first year, tools used, and the hiring steps. Do not invent salary, benefits or company claims.
2. **Pick a searchable title.** Use the title people actually search for ("Customer Support Specialist", not "Happiness Ninja"). Add the level if it matters (Junior, Senior, Lead).
3. **Write the role purpose** in 2-3 sentences: why the role exists and what success looks like.
4. **List responsibilities** as 5-8 bullets starting with verbs, ordered by time spent. Prefer outcomes ("Resolve 90% of tickets within one working day") over vague duties.
5. **Split requirements.** "Must have" holds only what a new hire truly needs on day one (max 5-6). Everything else goes to "Nice to have". Long must-have lists drive away qualified people who do not tick every box.
6. **State conditions plainly.** Pay range, hours, location, remote days, contract length, key benefits, travel. If the user will not publish pay, note that ads with pay usually get more relevant applicants, then respect the decision.
7. **Explain how to apply** and what happens next: documents needed, steps, expected timeline, contact (e.g. jobs@example.com).
8. **Run the fairness check** below, then read it aloud once for tone and length (aim for 300-600 words).

## Fairness checklist
- No age, gender, marital status, family plans, religion, ethnicity, nationality, health or disability requirements unless a real, lawful job requirement (check local labour and equal-treatment law).
- Avoid coded words: "young and dynamic team", "digital native", "recent graduate" (unless it is a graduate scheme), "native speaker" (say "fluent in English" instead), "he/she" (use "you").
- Years of experience only when truly needed; describe the skill instead.
- Physical requirements only if the job demands them, stated as tasks ("lift boxes up to 20 kg").
- Add an equal-opportunity line and offer adjustments for the hiring process.
- Include a short privacy note on how applicant data is used and kept.

## Template
```
[Job title] - [Location / Remote] - [Full-time / Part-time]

About the role
[2-3 sentences: purpose and what success looks like]

What you will do
- [verb + outcome]
- ...

What you need
- [must-have, max 5-6]

Nice to have
- [optional skills]

What we offer
- Pay: [range, gross/net, per month/year]
- [hours, remote, benefits]

How to apply
Send [CV / short note / portfolio] to jobs@example.com by [date].
Steps: [screening call] -> [interview] -> [task] -> [offer]. We reply within [X] days.
We welcome applicants of every background and will adjust the process on request.
```

## Example
Before: "Rockstar sales guy, max 30, native English, 10+ years experience."
After: "Sales Representative. You will win and look after 40-60 business clients. You need 2+ years in B2B sales and fluent English."

## Pitfalls
- Copying an internal job profile full of jargon and acronyms.
- A wish list of ten tools; keep the ones used daily.
- Promising things the company has not confirmed (bonus, remote, promotion).
- Requirements that clash with the stated level (junior role, senior demands).
- Legal rules on what an ad may contain differ by country; when unsure, ask an HR or legal professional.

## Output
Hand back the finished job description in the template, a short list of facts still missing, and any wording you changed for fairness with the reason.
