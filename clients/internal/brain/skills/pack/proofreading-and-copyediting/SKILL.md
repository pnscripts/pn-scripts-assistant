# Proofreading and copyediting

Fix what is wrong, not what is merely different from your taste. The author's meaning, voice and structure stay; errors and inconsistencies go.

## Steps
1. **Agree the level of edit.** Ask or infer:
   - *Proofread*: typos, spelling, grammar, punctuation, formatting only.
   - *Light copyedit*: the above plus consistency, awkward phrasing, word repetition.
   - *Heavy edit*: restructuring and rewriting (hand over to a rewrite skill instead).
   Also confirm the language variant (US/UK English) and any house style guide.
2. **Read once for sense** without editing. Note the audience, the purpose and terms that must not change (names, product names, quotes, legal wording).
3. **Pass 1 - mechanics.** Spelling, typos, doubled words, missing words, agreement (subject-verb, pronoun), tense, punctuation, apostrophes (its/it's), capitalisation.
4. **Pass 2 - consistency.** Build a mini style sheet as you go and apply it everywhere:
   - spelling variant (organise/organize), hyphenation (e-mail/email), capitalised terms
   - numbers (ten vs 10), dates (12 March 2026 vs 03/12/2026), currency, units
   - serial comma, quote marks, list punctuation, heading case
5. **Pass 3 - clarity, lightly.** Cut obvious redundancy ("in order to" -> "to"), fix ambiguous pronouns, split sentences over about 35 words only when they are hard to follow.
6. **Check facts you can see.** Totals that do not add up, dates that clash with weekdays, names spelled two ways, broken cross-references ("see section 4" when there is no section 4). Flag, do not guess.
7. **Return the result** in the agreed format (see Output).

## Checklist before handing back
- [ ] Meaning unchanged; no new claims added.
- [ ] Every proper noun spelled the same way throughout.
- [ ] Numbers, dates and units follow one style.
- [ ] Headings and lists are parallel in form.
- [ ] Quotes and legal or technical wording left exactly as written (only flagged).
- [ ] Final read from start to end after all fixes.

## Example
Original: "Our team have reviewed the the proposal and we beleive its ready, however we need the budget by Friday 14 March."
Edited: "Our team has reviewed the proposal and we believe it is ready. However, we need the budget by Friday, 14 March."
Notes: fixed doubled "the", spelling of "believe", its -> it is, comma splice split into two sentences. Query: is 14 March really a Friday this year?

## Pitfalls
- Rewriting style you simply dislike; that is not proofreading.
- "Correcting" deliberate choices: brand spellings, dialect, quoted speech, poetry.
- Introducing a new error while fixing another; re-read every edited sentence.
- Silently changing numbers or names you only suspect are wrong; query them instead.
- Mixing US and UK spelling because the tool's default differs from the author's.

## Output
Give: (1) the corrected text, clean and ready to use; (2) a short list of the notable changes grouped by type; (3) queries the author must decide (suspected factual errors, ambiguous meaning). For long texts, offer a tracked-changes style list ("original -> corrected") instead of only the clean text.
