# Employee onboarding

The first weeks decide whether a new hire becomes productive and stays. Make them predictable, welcoming and focused on one early win.

## Steps
1. **Collect the basics.** Role, start date, manager, team, location or remote, working hours, equipment needed, systems to access, and the 2-3 results expected by day 90.
2. **Before day one (preboarding).**
   - Contract signed and employment registered as local law requires; check current rules or ask an HR professional or accountant.
   - Equipment ordered, accounts created with only the access the role needs.
   - Welcome email sent 3-5 days ahead: time, place or link, who meets them, what to bring, dress code, first-week outline.
   - Buddy chosen (a peer, not the manager) and told what is expected.
3. **Day one.** Welcome, a tour (or virtual tour), working setup, meet the team, lunch or coffee with the buddy, the manager explains the role and the 30-60-90 plan. Finish with a short "how was today?" chat. Keep forms to a minimum.
4. **First week.** Mandatory items (safety, security, data protection, key policies), shadowing, first small real task that can be finished by Friday.
5. **30-60-90 plan.**
   - 30 days: learn the product, people and tools; deliver one small result.
   - 60 days: own a defined area with support.
   - 90 days: work independently on the agreed goals.
6. **Check-ins.** Short weekly 1:1 for the first month, then every two weeks. Formal review at the end of any probation period, with feedback both ways.
7. **Ask for feedback** on the onboarding itself at 30 and 90 days, and improve the checklist.

## Checklist
- [ ] Contract, registration and mandatory documents done (per local labour law)
- [ ] Laptop, phone, desk or home-office setup ready on day one
- [ ] Accounts: email, chat, calendar, role tools; least privilege; password manager and 2FA set up
- [ ] Data-protection and confidentiality briefing; new hire knows how their own personal data is handled
- [ ] Health and safety briefing where required
- [ ] Buddy assigned; team told about the start
- [ ] 30-60-90 plan written and shared
- [ ] 1:1s in the calendar; probation review date set
- [ ] Accessibility or other adjustments asked about and arranged

## Welcome email template
```
Subject: Welcome to the team - your first day on [date]

Hi [Name],

We are glad you are joining us as [role]. Your first day is [date] at [time],
at [address / video link]. [Manager] will meet you, and [Buddy] will be your
go-to person for any question in the first weeks.

Plan for day one: [setup], [team intro], [lunch], [role and goals talk].
Please bring: [ID / documents, if needed].
Questions before then: [manager]@example.com.

See you soon,
[Manager]
```

## Example 30-day goal
Support specialist: "By day 30, answer tier-1 tickets on your own with a satisfaction score in line with the team average."

## Pitfalls
- A first day of paperwork and an empty calendar.
- No working laptop or access on day one.
- Giving full admin access "to save time".
- Leaving everything to the buddy; the manager owns onboarding.
- Collecting personal data the job does not need, or sharing it in open channels.
- Waiting until the probation review to give the first feedback.

## Output
Hand back a dated onboarding plan (preboarding, day one, week one, 30-60-90 goals), the checklist with owners, the welcome email ready to send, and the check-in schedule.
