# Video editing workflow

Move from raw clips to a finished video in a predictable order, so nothing is redone. Menus differ between editors and versions: describe the concept and tell the user to check their editor's current manual for the exact command.

## Steps
1. **Define the piece.** Purpose, audience, platform, target length, aspect ratio (16:9, 9:16, 1:1) and deadline. Write the message in one sentence.
2. **Organise files before importing.** One project folder with subfolders: `footage`, `audio`, `music`, `graphics`, `exports`, `project`. Copy cards fully, keep a backup on a second drive.
3. **Set the project correctly.** Timeline resolution and frame rate match the main footage (e.g. 1920x1080 at 25 fps, or 3840x2160 at 30 fps). Do not mix 25 and 30 fps without a reason.
4. **Make proxies** for 4K, HEVC or long-GOP footage on a slow computer; edit with proxies, export from originals.
5. **Log and select.** Watch everything once, mark good takes (favourites, markers, keywords), drop the rest into a "rejects" bin.
6. **Rough cut.** Build the story first: interview or voice-over spine, then main shots. Ignore transitions and colour now. Aim slightly longer than the target length.
7. **Fine cut.** Trim pauses and repetition, cut on action, add B-roll over jump cuts, check pacing. Cut to the target length.
8. **Sound.** Dialogue clean and levelled (around -12 to -6 dBFS peaks for speech is a common working range), music ducked under speech, room tone over gaps, short fades on every audio cut.
9. **Colour.** First correct (exposure, white balance, match shots), then grade for the look. Use scopes, not only your eyes.
10. **Graphics and captions.** Titles, lower thirds, subtitles (burned in for social, separate SRT for platforms that support them). Keep text inside safe areas.
11. **Review.** Watch the full video once without stopping, ideally on another screen and on phone speakers. Note fixes with timecodes.
12. **Export** with the platform's current recommended settings, and archive the project with media.

## Example structure (3-minute explainer)
- 0:00-0:10 hook: the problem or the result
- 0:10-0:30 context: who and why
- 0:30-2:30 three main points, each with B-roll
- 2:30-3:00 summary and one clear call to action

## Pitfalls
- Editing from a USB stick or a nearly full drive causes stutter and lost media.
- Changing the timeline frame rate late breaks edits and audio sync.
- Music with no licence gets videos muted or blocked; use licensed or royalty-free tracks and keep the licence.
- Colour graded on an uncalibrated, very bright screen looks wrong elsewhere.
- No backup until the end: one drive failure loses the whole project.
