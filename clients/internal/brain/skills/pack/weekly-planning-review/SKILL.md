# Weekly planning and review

Thirty to sixty minutes once a week to get back in control: empty the heads and inboxes, look back honestly, and choose a few outcomes that matter.

## Steps
1. **Collect.** Ask the user to dump everything open: to-do lists, notes, emails needing action, messages, ideas, promises made to others. Put it all in one list.
2. **Look back (5 minutes).**
   - What did I finish? What moved forward?
   - What did not happen and why (too big, interrupted, not important, unclear)?
   - What did I learn about my time and energy?
3. **Process the list.** For each item decide: do this week, schedule later, delegate, waiting for someone, someday/maybe, or delete. Turn vague items into a next physical action ("call the plumber" instead of "bathroom").
4. **Check the bigger picture.** Look at goals, projects and deadlines for the next 2-4 weeks. Which need a step this week?
5. **Choose 3-5 weekly outcomes**, written as finished states: "Quote sent to the client", not "work on quote". At least one should move an important but not urgent goal.
6. **Check capacity.** Count fixed meetings and commitments; most people have far fewer free hours than they think. Leave roughly 20-30% unplanned for surprises.
7. **Place outcomes in the calendar** as focus blocks, hardest work in the user's best energy hours.
8. **Prepare Monday:** the first task of the week is clear and small enough to start at once.

## Template
```
Week of [date]
Wins last week: ...
Did not happen (why): ...
Lesson: ...
Outcomes this week (3-5):
 1. ...   [block: Tue 9-11]
 2. ...   [block: Wed 14-16]
Waiting for: [person - item - since]
Not this week (parked): ...
First action Monday: ...
```

## Example
A freelancer with 14 open items and two deadlines picks: "Client A logo finals delivered", "Invoice for September sent", "Portfolio page updated". Parks the newsletter idea to someday/maybe, delegates bookkeeping to the accountant, and blocks Tuesday and Thursday mornings.

## Pitfalls
- Planning a week of 60 tasks; overplanning guarantees failure and guilt.
- Reviewing without deciding; every item gets a decision.
- Skipping the look-back, so the same mistakes repeat.
- Keeping lists in five places; one trusted list is the point.

## Output
The completed weekly template, the processed list grouped by decision, and the calendar blocks for the week's outcomes.
