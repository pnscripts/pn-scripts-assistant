# Debt payoff plan

Turn a list of debts into a clear month-by-month plan. This is general guidance, not investment advice. Do not recommend specific lenders or products. For consolidation, refinancing, insolvency, or debts already with collectors, refer the user to their bank, a licensed financial adviser or a free debt-advice service.

## Steps
1. **List every debt:** lender (generic label is fine), balance, interest rate (APR), minimum payment, due day, fees, any promotional rate end date.
2. **Find the monthly amount** available for debt from the budget (see `household-budget`): minimums plus an extra amount the user can sustain.
3. **Protect the basics first:** keep paying minimums on everything; keep a small starter emergency fund so new spending does not go on the card (see `emergency-fund-and-savings`).
4. **Choose the order:**
   - Avalanche: highest interest rate first; costs least overall.
   - Snowball: smallest balance first; quick wins help motivation.
   Explain both; let the user pick.
5. **Schedule:** pay the extra amount to the first target; when it is paid off, add its whole payment to the next target ("rolling" payment).
6. **Estimate** the payoff date and total interest for both orders, and show the difference. State that it is an estimate assuming fixed rates and no new borrowing.
7. **Reduce the cost:** ask the current lender about a lower rate or hardship plan; avoid new credit; turn off card auto-increase of limit if possible.
8. **Review monthly** and celebrate each debt cleared.

## Example
| Debt | Balance | APR | Minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card A | 1 800 | 24% | 60 |
| Personal loan | 4 500 | 11% | 140 |
| Card B | 600 | 19% | 25 |

Extra per month: 150. Avalanche: Card A first (60+150), then Card B, then the loan. Snowball: Card B first (25+150), then Card A, then the loan. Show the month each is cleared and the interest difference.

Monthly interest estimate per debt: `balance * APR / 12`.

## Warning signs: get professional help now
- Minimum payments take more than about a third of take-home pay, or you skip food, rent or utilities to pay debts.
- Borrowing to repay other debts, or using payday-style loans.
- Court letters, enforcement notices or collector calls.
- Anyone asking for an upfront fee to "erase" debts (often a scam; see `phishing-and-scam-check`).

## Pitfalls
- Paying extra on low-rate debt while high-rate cards grow.
- Consolidating and then running the cards up again.
- Ignoring fees and promotional rates that jump later.
- Treating the plan as financial advice on products.

## Output
Hand back: the debt table, the chosen order with a month-by-month schedule or summary, estimated payoff date and interest saved for each method, the next three actions, any warning signs found, and the note that this is not investment advice, with a pointer to a financial adviser or debt-advice service where needed.
