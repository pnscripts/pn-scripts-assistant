# Emergency fund and savings goals

Help the user build a cash cushion and fund concrete goals step by step. This is not investment advice: do not recommend specific funds, shares, crypto, banks or products. For investing or pensions, refer the user to a licensed financial adviser and to official consumer-finance information.

## Steps
1. **Know the essential monthly costs:** housing, utilities, food, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments (from `household-budget`).
2. **Set the emergency-fund target.** A common rule of thumb is 3-6 months of essential costs; more for irregular income, a single income, dependants or an unstable job; less as a first milestone. Start with a starter goal (e.g. one month) to build the habit.
3. **Decide the general place:** money that is safe, easy to reach within a few days, and separate from the everyday account so it is not spent by accident. Tell the user to compare the deposit guarantee, fees, access rules and interest on official sources; do not name institutions.
4. **Automate:** a standing transfer on payday. Even a small amount counts; increase it after each pay rise or paid-off debt.
5. **List goals** with amount, date and priority, and compute the monthly amount: `monthly = (target - already saved) / months left`.
6. **Order:** starter emergency fund, then high-interest debt (see `debt-payoff-plan`), then the full emergency fund, then other goals.
7. **Define "emergency" in advance:** job loss, urgent repairs, medical costs, essential travel. Not sales or holidays. Refill after use.
8. **Windfalls:** suggest splitting by priority (e.g. part to the fund, part to debt, part to enjoy) and give the user the choice.

## Example answer
> Your essential costs are about 1 500 a month, so 4 500-9 000 is a typical cushion. You can save 400 a month: put 300 into the emergency fund (first milestone of 1 500 in five months) and 100 into the car fund. For investing beyond that, talk to a financial adviser.

## Pitfalls
- Keeping the emergency fund in something that can lose value or takes long to sell.
- Saving whatever is "left over" at month end, which is usually nothing.
- One big pot for everything; separate pots make goals visible.
- Promises of guaranteed high returns: a classic scam sign (see `phishing-and-scam-check`).

## Questions to compare where to keep the fund
- Is the money covered by a statutory deposit guarantee, and up to what amount?
- How fast can I get it out, and is there a fee or lost interest for early access?
- What are the account fees?
- What is the interest after tax, and is it fixed or variable?
- Is it separate from my everyday card so I do not spend it by accident?
Check the answers on the provider's official documents and the regulator's consumer pages.

## Output
Hand back: the emergency-fund target and starter milestone, the goals table with monthly amounts, the automation plan, the order of priorities, and the reminder that this is not investment advice, with a pointer to a financial adviser for investing.
