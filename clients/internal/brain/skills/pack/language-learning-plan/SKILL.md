# Language learning plan

Progress comes from regular contact with understandable input plus regular speaking and writing practice. The plan must fit daily life, or it will not be kept.

## Steps
1. **Collect the inputs.** Target language, current level (self-assessed, test result or a description such as "can order food, cannot follow films"), the reason (travel, job, exam, family, move), deadline, minutes available per day, and preferred ways to learn.
2. **Place the level on the CEFR scale.** A1 basic phrases, A2 simple everyday exchanges, B1 handles travel and familiar topics, B2 discusses most topics with some fluency, C1 flexible and precise, C2 near-native. If unsure, describe the next level and ask which sounds out of reach.
3. **Set one concrete goal.** Example: "Hold a 10-minute conversation about work and family by March" or "Pass a B1 exam in June". Avoid "be fluent".
4. **Balance the week.** With about 30 minutes a day, a typical split:
   - Input (listening, reading at a comfortable level): 40 %
   - Vocabulary with spaced repetition: 20 %
   - Speaking (tutor, exchange partner, shadowing, talking to yourself): 25 %
   - Writing and grammar in context: 15 %
5. **Pick resources by level.** Beginners: a structured course or textbook plus a phrasebook-style app. Intermediate: graded readers, podcasts for learners, series with target-language subtitles. Advanced: native media, books, writing for feedback.
6. **Build a weekly routine** with fixed slots (for example, commute = podcast, lunch = cards, evening = 15 min speaking).
7. **Set milestones every 4–6 weeks**: a recorded 2-minute monologue, a short written text, a reading test. Compare recordings over time.
8. **Plan for exam requirements** if the goal is a certificate: tell the user to check the official exam body for the current format, dates and fees.

## Example routine (A2 → B1, 30 min/day, 5 months)
```
Mon  Podcast for learners 15 | cards 10 | write 5 sentences about the day
Tue  Textbook unit (grammar in context) 20 | cards 10
Wed  Online conversation 30 (tutor or exchange partner)
Thu  Graded reader 20 | cards 10
Fri  Shadowing a 2-min audio 15 | series episode with target subtitles (weekend)
Sat  Review the week's mistakes, record a 2-min monologue
Sun  Rest or something fun in the language
```

## Pitfalls
- Only app streaks; they rarely lead to conversation on their own.
- Content far above the level; aim for material where about 90–95 % is understood.
- Avoiding speaking until "ready"; start speaking early and accept mistakes.
- Studying grammar lists without using them in sentences.
- Changing method every week; keep one routine for at least a month before judging it.
- No plan for missed days; agree on a 5-minute minimum version for busy days.
- Promising fixed hours to reach a level; estimates vary widely by language, learner and intensity, so present them as rough ranges.

## Output
Hand back: current and target level, the goal sentence, a weekly routine table, a resource type list per skill, milestones with dates, and a short note on how to measure progress.
