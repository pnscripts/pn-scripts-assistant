# Pricing and promotions

Price from three angles - cost, competitors and value to the customer - and check every promotion against the margin it leaves.

## Steps
1. **Full unit cost.** Product or materials + packaging + payment fees + marketplace or platform fees + average shipping subsidy + allowance for returns. Ask for real numbers; mark assumptions.
2. **Key formulas** (prices without VAT unless stated):
   - Margin % = (price - cost) / price x 100
   - Markup % = (price - cost) / cost x 100
   - Break-even units = fixed costs / (price - variable cost per unit)
   - Extra sales needed for a discount to pay off = old margin / (old margin - discount) - 1, with margin and discount as % of price
3. **Market check.** List 3-5 comparable offers with price, what is included, delivery and reviews. Decide position: budget, middle or premium, and why.
4. **Value check.** What does the customer save or gain? Premium needs visible proof (quality, service, guarantee, brand).
5. **Recommend a price** with the resulting margin, and a psychological format that fits the brand (e.g. 24.90 vs 25).
6. **Plan promotions** with a goal (clear stock, acquire new customers, raise order value). Options: % off, fixed amount off, bundle, free shipping over a threshold, gift with purchase, loyalty discount.
7. **Model each promotion**: expected units, new margin, total profit vs no promotion.

## Example
Cost 12, price 30: margin 60%. A 20% discount (price 24) leaves margin 50% of the original price (12 of 24). Extra sales needed: 60 / (60 - 20) - 1 = 0.5, so 50% more units just to earn the same profit.
Alternative: free shipping over 50 raises the average order instead of cutting price.

| Promotion | Goal | Dates | Discount | Expected units | Margin after | Profit vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free shipping over 50 | Higher order value | 1-14 Nov | shipping | +15% orders over 50 | 52% | +8% |
| Bundle 3 for 2 | Clear stock | 20-30 Nov | 33% on bundle | +40% units | 40% | +3% |

## Checklist
- Every price covers all fees, shipping and returns.
- Each promotion has a goal, an end date and a profit estimate.
- Reference prices are genuine and follow current rules.
- Promotions do not overlap in ways that stack into a loss.

## Pitfalls
- Forgetting fees and returns makes "profitable" prices lose money.
- Constant discounts train customers to wait for sales.
- In the EU, an announced price reduction must show the lowest price of the prior 30 days as the reference (with some exceptions); fake "was" prices and false countdowns are unfair practices. Check current national consumer rules.
- Show consumer prices including VAT where required; consult an accountant for tax treatment.

## Output
A cost and margin table, the recommended price with reasoning, a promotion plan with the profit model for each option, and the legal checks for advertising the discount.
