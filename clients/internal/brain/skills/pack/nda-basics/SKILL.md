# NDA basics

Help the user understand or prepare a confidentiality agreement. This is not legal advice. Recommend a lawyer when the NDA contains non-compete or non-solicitation terms, large penalties, assignment of intellectual property, or protects something central to the user's business.

## Steps
1. **Clarify the purpose:** who discloses what to whom, and why (evaluating a partnership, hiring a contractor, investor talks). One-way if only one side shares; mutual if both do.
2. **Check the definition of confidential information.** It should be clear: marked documents, or information a reasonable person would see as confidential. Too broad ("all information of any kind") is hard to comply with.
3. **Check the standard exclusions.** Information that is public through no fault of the recipient, already known, independently developed, or received lawfully from a third party; and disclosure required by law or a court.
4. **Check permitted use:** only for the stated purpose. Check who else may see it (employees, advisers) and on what terms.
5. **Check duration:** how long the duty lasts after the agreement ends. A few years is common for business information; trade secrets may need longer protection. Make sure it is stated.
6. **Check return or destruction** of materials at the end, with a reasonable exception for automatic backups.
7. **Look for extras that do not belong in a plain NDA:** non-compete, transfer of ideas or IP, exclusivity, penalties. Flag them clearly.
8. **Check law, courts and signatures.**

## Checklist
- [ ] Parties and purpose stated
- [ ] One-way or mutual matches reality
- [ ] Clear definition with standard exclusions
- [ ] Use limited to the purpose; who may receive it
- [ ] Defined duration
- [ ] Return or destruction on request
- [ ] No hidden non-compete, IP transfer or penalties (or flagged)
- [ ] Governing law, signature and date

## Template outline (mutual NDA)
```
1. Parties  2. Purpose  3. Confidential Information (definition)
4. Exclusions  5. Obligations (use only for Purpose, protect with reasonable care,
   share only with people who need to know and are bound by similar duties)
6. Legally required disclosure (notify the other party if allowed)
7. Return or destruction  8. Duration (agreement term + N years after)
9. No licence or obligation to proceed  10. Governing law and courts  11. Signatures
```
Fill only with the user's facts; leave N and the law for the user and, ideally, a lawyer to decide.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming an NDA protects an idea that you then publish yourself.
- Signing an NDA that quietly gives the other side rights to your ideas.
- Sharing before the NDA is signed by both sides.
- No record of what was shared; keep a list with dates.

## Output
Hand back: a plain summary of the NDA, the filled checklist with clause numbers, flagged clauses and suggested wording, or a filled outline, plus the statement that this is not legal advice and when to see a lawyer.
