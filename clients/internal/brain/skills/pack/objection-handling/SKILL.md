# Objection handling

An objection is a question in disguise. Find the real concern, answer it honestly, and agree on a next step - or accept a clear no.

## Steps
1. **Listen and restate.** Repeat the objection in your own words to confirm it ("So the price is above what you planned for this quarter?").
2. **Find the real concern.** Ask one open question: "Compared with what?", "What would need to be true for this to work?", "Who else is involved in the decision?" The stated objection is often not the real one.
3. **Classify it.**
   - Price or budget
   - Timing ("not now")
   - Status quo ("we already use X")
   - Trust or risk ("never heard of you", "what if it fails")
   - Authority ("I need to ask my manager")
   - Brush-off ("send me information")
4. **Answer with evidence**, not pressure: a relevant result, a comparison, a guarantee or trial that actually exists, a smaller first step.
5. **Offer options.** A smaller package, phased payment, a pilot, a later start date. Only offer what the user can really deliver.
6. **Ask for a next step** with a date: a call with the decision maker, a trial start, a follow-up on a specific day.
7. **Know when to stop.** If there is no need, budget or fit, say thank you and leave the door open. A respectful exit protects reputation.

## Playbook template
| Objection | Real concern to check | Honest answer | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Too expensive." | Price vs. value, or no budget now? | "What are you comparing it with? Our clients usually save X hours a month, which pays back in about N months." | Offer the smaller plan or a payback calculation. |
| "We already have a supplier." | Satisfied or just used to them? | "Makes sense. What would you improve about the current setup if you could?" | Offer a side-by-side test on one task. |
| "Not now." | Timing or no priority? | "When does planning for this usually happen?" | Agree on a date to talk again. |
| "Send me information." | Interest or polite exit? | "Happy to. Which part matters most, so I send only what's useful?" | Send a one-page summary, book a 10-minute follow-up. |
| "I need to ask my boss." | Who decides and on what? | "What will your manager want to know? I can prepare it or join the call." | Joint call with the decision maker. |

## Example
Prospect: "Your quote is 30 % higher than the other offer."
Reply: "Thanks for being open about it. Can I ask what's included in the other offer? Ours includes setup and a year of support, which are often billed separately. If those are covered elsewhere, we could remove them and get closer to your budget."

## Pitfalls
- Arguing or proving the client wrong; you may win the point and lose the deal.
- Discounting at once; it teaches the client that the first price was not real.
- Answering the stated objection when the real one is different.
- False scarcity, invented deadlines or misleading comparisons with competitors.
- Talking more than listening; aim for the client to speak most of the time.

## Output
Return the objection playbook (table) or a tailored reply to the specific objection, the clarifying question to ask first, and the proposed next step. Flag any claim that the user must confirm is true before using it.
