# Prioritisation

Prioritising means choosing what not to do now. Make the choice explicit, based on goals and consequences, and communicate it.

## Steps
1. **Get the full list** and the context: goals for this period, deadlines, who asked for what, available time.
2. **Remove the noise.** Delete duplicates, merge related items, and drop anything that no longer serves a goal.
3. **Pick a method that fits:**
   - **Eisenhower** (personal tasks): urgent and important -> do now; important not urgent -> schedule; urgent not important -> delegate or minimise; neither -> drop.
   - **Impact / effort** (ideas, improvements): high impact + low effort first (quick wins); high impact + high effort planned as projects; low impact + high effort dropped.
   - **MoSCoW** (scope of a release or project): Must, Should, Could, Won't (this time).
   - **Cost of delay** (competing deadlines): what is lost each week this waits? Highest loss first.
4. **Score quickly** - 1-3 or 1-5 scales, no false precision. Ask the user to confirm scores for anything with a big consequence.
5. **Order and cut.** Produce the top 3-5 items for now. Everything else goes to "later" or "not doing", with a reason.
6. **Handle conflicts.** If two people's requests compete, the person who sets the goals decides; prepare a short message stating the trade-off.
7. **Renegotiate.** Draft polite messages for deadlines that must move or requests the user will decline.

## Example
List: client proposal (due Friday), tax filing (due in 10 days), website redesign idea, reply to 40 emails, team training plan.
- Do now: client proposal.
- Schedule: tax filing (Wednesday morning block), training plan (next week).
- Minimise: emails - 30 minutes twice a day, archive newsletters.
- Later: redesign idea, park it for the quarterly review.

## Saying no template
"Thanks for thinking of me. This week I am committed to [priority] for [goal/deadline]. I can [smaller alternative / start on date], or if this is more important, which of my current tasks should move?"

## Checklist
- Each top item is linked to a goal or a real deadline.
- The top list fits the time actually available this week.
- Deferred items have a date to look at them again.
- People affected by the choices have been told.

## Pitfalls
- Treating loud as urgent; ask what really happens if it waits.
- Ranking everything "high"; force a strict order.
- Choosing only quick wins; important long work never starts.
- Prioritising silently; people affected need to know.

## Output
An ordered list with the top 3-5 items for now, the deferred and dropped items with reasons, the method used, and draft messages for any renegotiation.
