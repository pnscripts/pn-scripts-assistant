# General workout plan

Build a simple, sustainable plan for a healthy adult. This is general information, not medical advice and not a diagnosis. Ask about health first; if anything below applies, tell the user to talk to a doctor before starting or increasing exercise.

## Screen first
Recommend checking with a doctor before starting if the user: has heart, lung, kidney or metabolic disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, a recent operation or injury, is pregnant or recently gave birth, takes regular medication, has had chest pain, fainting or dizziness during effort, or is older and has been inactive for a long time.

## Red flags: stop and seek urgent care (112 in the EU)
Chest pain or pressure, pain spreading to arm, jaw or back, severe shortness of breath, fainting, sudden weakness or numbness on one side, confusion, irregular racing heartbeat that does not settle. For sharp joint pain, swelling or pain that persists, stop that exercise and see a doctor or physiotherapist.

## Steps
1. **Goal and constraints:** goal (general health, strength, endurance, weight management, mobility), days and minutes available, place (home, gym, outdoors), equipment, current activity level, preferences and dislikes.
2. **Use public guidance as the frame.** Widely published health guidance for adults suggests a weekly total of moderate aerobic activity (often quoted as at least 150 minutes) plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days; less is still better than none. Tell the user to check current guidance from their national health authority or the WHO.
3. **Build the week:** spread cardio over several days; 2-3 full-body strength sessions on non-consecutive days; short mobility work; at least one rest day.
4. **Choose exercises** by movement pattern: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, core. Offer a no-equipment version of each.
5. **Set intensity by feel:** moderate means you can talk but not sing; strength sets end with 2-3 good repetitions still in reserve.
6. **Progress slowly:** add a little (time, repetitions or load) once a week only if the previous week felt manageable and pain-free.
7. **Warm up and cool down:** 5-10 minutes easy movement before, gentle walking and stretching after.
8. **Track** sessions in a simple log and review after 4 weeks.

## Example: 3 days, 30 minutes, home, beginner
| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | 5 min warm-up; 3 rounds: 10 chair squats, 8 wall or knee push-ups, 10 glute bridges, 8 bent-over rows with water bottles, 20 s plank; 5 min stretch |
| Wed | 25-30 min brisk walk or cycling at "can talk, can't sing" pace |
| Fri | Repeat Monday; add 1-2 repetitions per exercise if Monday felt easy |
| Daily | 10 min walk after a meal; 5 min mobility (hips, shoulders, ankles) |

## Pitfalls
- Doing too much in week one, then quitting from soreness or injury.
- Training through pain, dizziness or chest symptoms.
- Copying an advanced athlete's programme.
- Ignoring sleep and rest days, where progress actually happens.
- Giving specific advice for a medical condition; that belongs to a doctor or physiotherapist.

## Progress log template
| Date | Session | What I did | Effort (1-10) | Pain or symptoms | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Strength | 3 rounds, all exercises | 6 | none | squats felt easy |

Any pain or symptom recorded twice in a row is a reason to ease off and, if it persists, see a doctor.

## Output
Hand back: the health-screen questions and any reason to see a doctor first, the weekly table with exercises, sets or minutes and no-equipment options, the progression rule, the red-flag list, and a 4-week review point.
