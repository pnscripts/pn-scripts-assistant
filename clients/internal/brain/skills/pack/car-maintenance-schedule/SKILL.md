# Car maintenance schedule

The owner's manual (service booklet) is the authority. Use typical intervals only as orientation and adjust for severe use: short trips, towing, dusty roads, city traffic, extreme heat or cold.

## Steps
1. **Collect the facts.** Make, model, year, engine and fuel (petrol, diesel, hybrid, electric), gearbox, current mileage, service history, how the car is used (short city trips, long motorway, towing) and climate.
2. **Get the manufacturer schedule.** Ask the user for the manual or service booklet, or tell them to check the manufacturer's official site or a dealer. If unavailable, use the typical intervals below and label them as orientation.
3. **List what is overdue or unknown.** For a used car with unclear history, treat oil, filters, brake fluid and coolant as "due now", and check whether a timing belt change is required and when it was last done.
4. **Build the schedule** by kilometres and by time, whichever comes first.
5. **Add legal deadlines**: periodic technical inspection, compulsory insurance, road tax or vignette. Tell the user to confirm current dates and rules with the official authority in their country.
6. **Set reminders** in the calendar and create a service log.
7. **Add monthly owner checks** (5 minutes, engine cold, on level ground).

## Typical intervals (orientation only; the manual wins)
| Item | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Engine oil and filter | every 10,000–30,000 km or 12 months (shorter for severe use) |
| Air filter | 20,000–40,000 km |
| Cabin (pollen) filter | 15,000–30,000 km or yearly |
| Brake fluid | every 2 years |
| Coolant | per manual, often 4–5 years for long-life coolant |
| Spark plugs (petrol) | 30,000–100,000 km depending on type |
| Timing belt (if fitted) | per manual, often 60,000–160,000 km or 5–10 years; a failure can destroy the engine |
| Brake pads and discs | inspect at every service; replace by wear |
| Tyres | replace by tread depth or age (often checked after about 6 years); legal minimum depth varies by country |
| 12 V battery | test yearly after 3–4 years |
| Electric car | tyres, brakes, cabin filter, coolant for battery, brake fluid; fewer items but not none |

## Monthly owner checks
- Tyre pressure (cold, value from the door frame sticker or manual) and visible damage.
- Oil level on the dipstick or display; coolant and washer fluid levels.
- All lights and indicators; wiper blades.
- Leaks under the car; new noises, vibrations or warning lights.

## Service log template
```
Date | km | Work done | Parts/fluids (spec) | Garage | Cost | Next due (km/date)
```

## Pitfalls
- Using the wrong oil specification; it must match the manual (viscosity and approval code).
- Skipping time-based items on low-mileage cars; fluids and rubber age anyway.
- Ignoring the timing belt on a used car with no proof of replacement.
- Topping up coolant or opening the radiator cap on a hot engine; it can scald.
- Doing brake or airbag work without proper training; these are safety-critical, use a qualified mechanic.

## Output
Hand back: the overdue/due-now list, a schedule table (item, interval by km and time, next due), legal deadlines to confirm, the monthly checklist and a service log template.
