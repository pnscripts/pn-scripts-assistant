# GDPR basics

Give clear, general information about the EU General Data Protection Regulation. This is not legal advice: for disputes, large-scale or sensitive processing, or a serious breach, tell the user to see a lawyer or a data-protection specialist, and point to the national supervisory authority for official guidance.

## Key ideas
- **Personal data:** anything that identifies a person directly or indirectly (name, email, IP address, photo, customer number).
- **Special categories** (health, religion, biometrics, etc.) need extra protection; flag them and recommend professional help.
- **Controller** decides why and how data is used; **processor** acts on the controller's instructions (e.g. a hosting or email provider) under a written agreement.
- **Lawful basis:** every use needs one of six bases: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests. Consent is not always required and is not always the best basis.
- **Principles:** collect only what is needed, for a stated purpose, keep it accurate, keep it no longer than needed, keep it secure, be able to show compliance.

## Steps for a person exercising rights
1. Identify the controller (the company's privacy notice names it and a contact).
2. Choose the right: access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, portability, withdrawing consent.
3. Send a written request through the contact in the privacy notice (verify the address on the official site, never from an unsolicited message).
4. Keep a dated copy. The controller normally must answer within one month (it may extend in complex cases and must say why).
5. If there is no answer or a refusal you disagree with, complain to the supervisory authority.

### Template: access request
```
Subject: Data access request under Article 15 GDPR
Hello, I ask for a copy of the personal data you hold about me, the purposes,
recipients, retention period and source. Account email: name@example.com.
Please reply within one month. Thank you, [Name]
```

## Steps for a small organisation
1. List what personal data you hold, where, why, on which basis, and for how long (a simple table is enough to start).
2. Remove what you do not need.
3. Publish a clear privacy notice (see `privacy-policy-review`).
4. Sign processor agreements with service providers that handle the data.
5. Secure access: strong passwords, 2FA, least privilege, backups.
6. Prepare a breach routine: assess risk, record it, and notify the authority within 72 hours when required, and affected people when the risk is high.
7. Set up a way to handle rights requests on time.

## Pitfalls
- Pre-ticked consent boxes or bundling consent with terms.
- Buying email lists for marketing.
- Keeping data "just in case" forever.
- Assuming GDPR does not apply because the business is small or free of charge.
- Treating this summary as a substitute for advice in a dispute.
