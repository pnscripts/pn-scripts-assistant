# Lesson planning

Plan backwards: decide what learners must be able to do at the end, how you will see it, and only then choose activities.

## Steps
1. **Gather the context.** Subject and topic, age or grade, group size, lesson length, prior knowledge, curriculum requirement if any, available materials and room (projector, devices, outdoors), and learners with special needs.
2. **Write 1–3 learning objectives** as observable actions: "By the end, learners can compare…, calculate…, explain…". Avoid "understand" or "know about".
3. **Decide the evidence.** How will you know each objective is met? An exit ticket, a short task, a quick quiz, a product, a spoken explanation.
4. **Build the sequence** (adapt the timing to the lesson length):
   - Hook (5 min): a question, picture, puzzle or short story that creates curiosity.
   - Activate prior knowledge (5 min): quick recall or brainstorm.
   - New content (10–15 min): short explanation with modelling ("I do").
   - Guided practice (10 min): learners try with support ("we do").
   - Independent practice (10–15 min): individual or pair task ("you do").
   - Check and close (5 min): exit ticket, summary in their own words, preview of next lesson.
5. **Differentiate.** Plan support (sentence starters, worked examples, partner) and stretch (harder problem, "explain why", open task) for the same objective.
6. **List materials and preparation** with quantities.
7. **Plan checks for understanding** during the lesson: cold-call questions, mini whiteboards, thumbs up/down, "turn and talk".
8. **Add homework only if it practises the objective** and can be done without help.

## Template
```
Topic: Fractions - equivalent fractions | Grade 4 | 45 min | 24 pupils
Objectives: pupils can (1) show two equivalent fractions with a model, (2) find one by multiplying.
Evidence: exit ticket - 3 items.
0-5    Hook: "Is half a pizza the same as 4/8 of a pizza?" vote and discuss
5-15   Model with paper strips: 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8
15-25  Pairs fold strips, record pairs of equivalent fractions
25-38  Worksheet: 6 items (support: strips; stretch: find 3 equivalents of 3/5)
38-45  Exit ticket + "one thing I learned"
Materials: 24 sets of paper strips, worksheet copies, sticky notes
```

## Pitfalls
- Too much content for the time; plan less and include a "if time remains" extension.
- Activities chosen because they are fun, not because they serve the objective.
- Long teacher talk; keep explanation segments under about 10–15 minutes for younger learners.
- No check before moving on; misunderstandings grow.
- Ignoring safety for practical work (labs, cooking, outdoors); list precautions.
- Copying curriculum codes from memory; ask the teacher to confirm the exact current standard.

## Output
Hand back: objectives, success evidence, a timed lesson sequence with teacher and learner actions, differentiation notes, materials list, checks for understanding, and optional homework or exit-ticket questions.
