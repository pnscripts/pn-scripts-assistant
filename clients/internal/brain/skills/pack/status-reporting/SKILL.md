# Status reporting

A status report lets a busy reader know in 30 seconds whether to worry and what they need to do. Lead with the truth, especially when it is bad news.

## Steps
1. **Collect inputs:** plan and milestones, tasks done and planned, current risks and issues, budget spent vs planned, any decisions pending. Ask who the reader is.
2. **Set the overall status** honestly:
   - Green - on track for scope, time and budget.
   - Amber - at risk; a recovery plan exists and is within the team's control.
   - Red - will miss a milestone, budget or quality target without help or a decision.
   Explain the colour in one sentence. Never keep "green" while a key milestone is clearly slipping.
3. **Write the headline first:** status, the most important news, and the decision needed, if any.
4. **Progress:** milestones with planned vs forecast date. Show changes since last report.
5. **Done this period / next period:** 3-5 bullets each, outcomes rather than activities ("supplier contract signed", not "worked on contract").
6. **Risks and issues:** only the top 3, with owner and action.
7. **Decisions and help needed:** what, from whom, by when, and the consequence of no decision.
8. **Budget** if relevant: spent, forecast at completion, variance.
9. **Adjust the length** to the reader: executive - 5 lines; team - full template; client - outcomes and next steps, no internal blame.

## Template
```
Project: [name] - Status report [date]          Overall: AMBER
Headline: [one or two sentences: where we are and what is needed]
Milestones:  [name] planned [date] -> forecast [date] [on track / late]
Done:        - ...
Next:        - ...
Risks/issues: - [risk] - owner - action
Decisions needed: - [decision] from [who] by [date]; if not: [impact]
Budget: spent [x] of [y]; forecast [z]
```

## Example headline for bad news
"AMBER: Launch forecast moves from 3 to 10 June because payment-provider approval is late. We need a go/no-go by Thursday on launching without card payments (bank transfer only) to keep 3 June."

## Pitfalls
- Long activity lists that hide the one thing that matters.
- Watermelon reports: green outside, red inside.
- Surprises: if it will be red next week, say amber this week.
- Blaming people by name in reports to clients or executives; describe the cause and the fix.

## Output
The status report in the template, a one-line headline suitable for chat or email subject, and a note of any facts that were missing.
