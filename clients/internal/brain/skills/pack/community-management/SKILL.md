# Community management

Reply like a helpful person, not a press office. Protect members, stay calm in public, and move private details to private channels.

## Steps
1. **Read the full context**: the comment, the thread, the person's history with the brand, any order or case details the user has.
2. **Classify** the message:
   - question -> answer or point to the right resource
   - praise -> thank, personalise, maybe ask to share
   - complaint (fair) -> acknowledge, apologise for the experience, fix, follow up
   - complaint (unclear) -> ask for details in a private message
   - troll or abuse -> do not feed; moderate per the rules
   - spam, scam, illegal content, threats -> remove, report, keep evidence
3. **Draft the reply** using the ARF pattern: Acknowledge the feeling, Respond with facts or action, Follow up with the next step or contact. Keep it short, use the person's name, sign with a first name if the brand does.
4. **Move private data off public threads.** Never ask for or post order numbers with personal details, addresses, phone numbers or card data in public.
5. **Escalate** to the right owner when it involves safety, legal threats, media, data breaches, health or many similar complaints at once. Pause scheduled promotional posts during a crisis.
6. **Log** recurring questions and complaints; they feed the FAQ and product fixes.

## Community guidelines template
1. Be respectful - no harassment, hate speech or personal attacks.
2. Stay on topic; no spam or unsolicited ads.
3. No sharing of others' personal information.
4. No illegal content or scams.
5. Moderators may hide or remove posts and remove members who break these rules.
6. Report problems to the moderators: [how].

## Example
Comment: "Ordered two weeks ago, still nothing. Terrible service."
Reply: "Hi Maria, sorry - two weeks is too long and we understand the frustration. We have sent you a private message to check your order right away. - Elena"

## Pitfalls
- Deleting fair criticism usually backfires; answer it instead.
- Arguing in public, sarcasm and copy-paste replies make things worse.
- Never offer compensation publicly that you cannot give to everyone in the same situation.
- Check each platform's current moderation tools and reporting rules.

## Output
The classification, a reply draft (public and, if needed, private), the moderation or escalation action, and a note if the issue should go into the FAQ or a product fix list.
