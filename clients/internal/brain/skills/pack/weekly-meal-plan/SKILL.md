# Weekly meal plan

A plan that is cooked beats a perfect plan that is not. Fit it to the household's real evenings, budget and tastes.

## Steps
1. **Collect the facts.** Number of people and ages, which meals to plan (dinners only, or lunches too), diets and allergies, strong dislikes, cooking time per day, budget level, kitchen equipment (oven, slow cooker, air fryer), what is already in the fridge, freezer and pantry, and busy evenings.
2. **Set the frame.** Mark busy days for 15–20 minute meals or leftovers, and one longer cooking slot (often the weekend) for batch cooking.
3. **Build balanced plates.** Aim for roughly half vegetables or salad, a quarter protein (beans, lentils, eggs, fish, poultry, meat), a quarter whole grains or starchy food, plus some fruit and dairy or alternatives through the day. Vary protein sources across the week.
4. **Plan ingredient overlap.** Use the same base ingredient twice in different ways (roast chicken Monday → chicken wraps Tuesday; a pot of tomato sauce → pasta and shakshuka).
5. **Use what is on hand first**, especially items close to their date.
6. **Write the plan** with a prep note for each day ("defrost fish in the fridge the night before").
7. **Generate the shopping list** grouped by store section, with quantities, and mark items already at home.
8. **Add a flex night**: leftovers, eggs on toast or a freezer meal for when plans change.

## Template
```
        Dinner                              Prep note
Mon     Lentil and vegetable soup + bread   Make double; freeze 2 portions
Tue     Oven chicken thighs, potatoes, salad Roast 2 extra thighs
Wed     Chicken and pepper wraps (leftovers) 15 min
Thu     Pasta with tomato sauce and tuna    Use the sauce batch
Fri     Flex: leftovers or omelette
Sat     Homemade pizza with the kids
Sun     Baked fish, rice, green beans       Cook rice for Monday lunch

Shopping list
Produce:  onions 1 kg, carrots 500 g, peppers 3, salad 2 heads, lemons 2
Protein:  chicken thighs 1.2 kg, tuna 2 cans, white fish 600 g, eggs 10
Pantry:   red lentils 500 g, pasta 500 g, rice 1 kg, canned tomatoes 3
Dairy:    mozzarella 250 g, plain yogurt 500 g
Have:     olive oil, spices, flour
```

## Food safety basics
- Cool cooked food quickly and refrigerate within about 2 hours; eat refrigerated leftovers within about 3 days or freeze them.
- Thaw in the fridge, not on the counter; reheat until steaming hot throughout.
- Keep raw meat below ready-to-eat food in the fridge.

## Pitfalls
- Seven new, complex recipes in one week; reuse favourites and add one new dish.
- Ignoring the calendar; a slow recipe on a training night will be skipped.
- Buying fresh produce for the whole week at once; plan delicate items early in the week.
- Forgetting breakfasts, snacks and packed lunches if the user expects them.
- Giving medical diets (diabetes, kidney disease, severe allergies) as if you were a dietitian; suggest the user confirm with their doctor or dietitian.

## Output
Hand back: a 7-day table (meal plus prep note), a grouped shopping list with quantities and "already have" items, batch-cooking and leftover notes, and one or two swaps for picky eaters or a tighter budget.
