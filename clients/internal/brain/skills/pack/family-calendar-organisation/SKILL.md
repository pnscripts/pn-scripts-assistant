# Family calendar organisation

The goal is one calendar everyone trusts, where every event says who is responsible, and a short weekly routine that keeps it current.

## Steps
1. **Collect the facts.** Household members and ages, devices and calendar apps already used, work patterns and shifts, school and nursery times, regular activities, shared custody arrangements if any, and recurring household events (bins, bills, medicines).
2. **Pick one shared tool** that works on everyone's devices (the calendar app already on their phones is usually best). Avoid a second paper calendar unless it is clearly a copy for younger children.
3. **Structure it.**
   - One shared "Family" calendar plus each person's own calendar visible to the others, or one colour per person.
   - Naming rule: `[who] what - where`, for example `[Mia] Swimming - city pool`.
   - Put the responsible adult in the event: `Drop: Dad / Pick: Mum`.
4. **Enter recurring items first**: school days and holidays, activities, work shifts, bin days, medicines, birthdays.
5. **Add reminders with lead time**: departure reminder (travel time + 10 min), evening-before reminder for kit and forms, one week before for birthdays and paperwork.
6. **Add travel and buffer** around activities so back-to-back events are visible.
7. **Run a 15-minute weekly check-in** (for example Sunday evening): review next week, assign every pick-up, note meals for busy evenings, add new letters and invitations, and check conflicts.
8. **Share access safely.** Give older children view or edit access as appropriate; keep private details (addresses, medical notes) out of calendars shared with people outside the household.

## Weekly overview template
```
Week of ____      Mum            Dad            Mia (9)               Leo (5)
Mon             office 9-17     home office    school 8-14, piano 16  nursery 8-16
                                              Pick: Dad 14:00        Pick: Dad 16:00
Tue             late 19:00      office         school, football 17   nursery
                                              Drop/Pick: Dad          Pick: Grandma
...
To do: sign school trip form (Wed), buy gift for Sat party, dentist Leo 12th
```

## Conflict rules
- A clash is resolved at the weekly check-in, not by the first person who notices.
- If nobody is assigned, it is not covered: mark "NEEDS DRIVER" in the title.
- Keep a short backup list (grandparent, neighbour, carpool family) with consent.

## Pitfalls
- Two calendars that drift apart.
- Events without a responsible person.
- Too many notifications, so everyone ignores them; keep reminders purposeful.
- Forgetting school holidays, teacher training days and term dates; ask the user to copy them from the official school calendar.
- Putting sensitive information (door codes, health details) in shared event notes.

## Output
Hand back: the recommended calendar structure (calendars, colours, naming rule), the list of recurring events to enter, reminder settings, a weekly overview table for the coming week with assignments and conflicts flagged, and a weekly check-in agenda.
