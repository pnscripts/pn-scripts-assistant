# Research brief

Turn a vague topic into a clear question, collect good sources, and hand back a brief someone can act on in five minutes. Separate what is established from what is uncertain.

## Steps
1. **Define the question.** Restate it in one sentence with scope: who, where, which period, what decision it supports. Ask one clarifying question only if the scope is truly unclear.
2. **Break it into sub-questions.** Three to six, e.g. "What does it cost?", "What are the risks?", "What do experts disagree on?".
3. **Plan the sources.** For each sub-question, pick the best source type: official statistics and regulators, peer-reviewed reviews, standards bodies, reputable news, industry reports, primary documents. Note what to search for.
4. **Search and collect.** Use precise search terms, synonyms and the local language when the topic is local. Save title, publisher, date and link for each useful source. Aim for quality over count, usually 5-12 sources.
5. **Evaluate.** Rate each source (see the source-evaluation skill). Drop weak ones or label them.
6. **Synthesise.** For each sub-question, write the answer in 2-4 sentences, citing sources. Where sources disagree, say so and explain why.
7. **Assign confidence.** High (several strong sources agree), medium (limited or mixed evidence), low (little or weak evidence).
8. **List gaps** and what would answer them (a call, a dataset, an expert).

## Checklist
- [ ] Question and scope written down
- [ ] Every finding has a source with a date
- [ ] Disagreements are shown, not hidden
- [ ] Facts and opinions are separated
- [ ] Time-sensitive facts are marked "as of <date>"

## Template
```
Research brief: <question>
Scope: <region, period, purpose>
Bottom line: <2-3 sentences>

Findings
1. <sub-question> - <answer> [1][2] (confidence: high)
2. ...

Disagreements and uncertainty: ...
Open questions: ...
Sources: [1] Publisher, "Title", date, link
```

## Example
Question: "Should our small office switch to a heat pump?" Sub-questions: running cost vs current heating, installation cost and grants, suitability of the building, noise and maintenance. Bottom line gives conditions under which it pays off, with each figure dated and sourced.

## Pitfalls
- Searching for confirmation of a preferred answer; also search for the counter-argument.
- Treating the first page of results as the whole picture.
- Mixing data from different years or countries without saying so.
- Overlong briefs; put detail in an appendix, keep the bottom line on top.

## Output
Hand back the brief in the template: bottom line first, sourced findings with confidence, disagreements, open questions and the full source list.
