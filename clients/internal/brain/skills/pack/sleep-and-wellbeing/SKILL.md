# Sleep and wellbeing

Share practical, general habits that help most adults sleep and cope with everyday stress. This is not medical advice: do not diagnose sleep or mental-health conditions and do not suggest sleeping pills, supplements or doses. Tell the user to see a doctor when problems persist or any warning sign below appears.

## Get help now
- Thoughts of suicide or self-harm, or feeling unable to stay safe: call 112 (EU emergency number) or a local crisis line now, or go to the nearest emergency department. Stay with the user in the conversation and encourage contacting someone they trust.
- Falling asleep while driving or at dangerous moments.

## See a doctor soon if
Loud snoring with pauses in breathing or gasping, sleep problems most nights for several weeks, daytime sleepiness that affects work or safety, low mood or loss of interest lasting two weeks or more, panic attacks, restless or jerking legs at night, or sleep problems that started with a new medication.

## Steps
1. **Understand the pattern:** usual bed and wake times on work and free days, time to fall asleep, night waking, how rested they feel, caffeine, alcohol, naps, screens, exercise, shift work, stress.
2. **Keep a 1-2 week sleep diary** (template below) before changing much; patterns often surprise.
3. **Anchor the wake time:** the same time every day, including weekends, is the strongest single habit. Morning daylight helps set the body clock.
4. **Wind down for 30-60 minutes:** dim lights, no work email, a calm routine (reading, shower, stretching).
5. **Bedroom:** dark, quiet, cool, comfortable; the bed for sleep, not for work or scrolling.
6. **Timing:** caffeine well before the afternoon ends, no heavy meals or much alcohol close to bedtime, exercise earlier in the day if late exercise disturbs sleep.
7. **If awake for a long time,** get up, do something calm in dim light, return when sleepy; do not watch the clock.
8. **For stress:** a few minutes of slow breathing (e.g. breathe in for 4, out for 6), writing a "worry list" and tomorrow's plan in the evening, a short daily walk, and regular contact with people.
9. **Review after two weeks;** if no improvement or warning signs appear, see a doctor.

## Sleep diary template
| Date | Bed time | Time to fall asleep | Wake-ups | Wake time | Rested (1-5) | Caffeine / alcohol / naps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 23:30 | ~40 min | 2 | 07:00 | 2 | coffee 16:00 |

## Pitfalls
- Sleeping in at weekends to "catch up", which shifts the body clock.
- Trying hard to sleep; effort keeps the brain alert.
- Using alcohol as a sleep aid; it fragments sleep.
- Presenting tips as treatment for insomnia, depression or anxiety; those need a doctor.

## Example evening routine (wake time 07:00)
- 21:30 lights dimmed, work and news apps closed
- 22:00 shower, prepare clothes and bag for tomorrow, write the worry list
- 22:30 read a paper book in bed, phone charging outside the bedroom
- 23:00 lights off; if still awake after a long time, get up and read in dim light
- 07:00 get up, open the curtains or go outside for daylight

## Output
Hand back: a short summary of the user's pattern, 3-5 specific changes with times, the diary template, the list of warning signs and when to see a doctor, and crisis contacts if any risk was mentioned.
