# Marketing copywriting

Good copy tells one reader what they get, why to believe it and what to do next. Clarity beats cleverness; proof beats adjectives.

## Steps
1. **Brief first.** Ask for: product, one target reader, their main problem, the single action wanted (buy, sign up, book), proof available (numbers, reviews, guarantees), tone, channel and length limits.
2. **Find the core message.** Finish this sentence: "For [reader] who [problem], [product] [main result] because [reason to believe]." If you cannot, ask more questions.
3. **Turn features into benefits.** For each feature ask "so what?" until you reach something the reader cares about.
4. **Pick a structure** that fits the channel:
   - Landing page: headline, sub-headline, 3 benefits, proof, how it works, objections/FAQ, call to action.
   - Ad: hook, benefit, proof or offer, call to action.
   - PAS (problem, agitate, solution) for pain-driven offers; AIDA for longer pages.
5. **Write 3 headline options** in different angles (outcome, problem, curiosity/specific number), then the body.
6. **Tighten.** Cut filler ("innovative", "world-class", "solutions"), use the reader's words, short sentences, active verbs, one idea per paragraph.
7. **Check claims.** Every number, "best", "fastest" or "guaranteed" needs proof the user can show. Mark unsupported claims and soften them.

## Example
Feature: "Automatic daily backups."
- Weak: "Our innovative backup solution offers best-in-class reliability."
- Better: "Lost a file? Restore yesterday's version in two clicks - backups run every night, automatically."

Call to action: "Start free trial" beats "Submit"; say what happens next ("No card needed").

## Checklist
- Is the headline understandable in 5 seconds by someone who never heard of the product?
- Does it say who it is for?
- Is there one clear primary call to action?
- Is every claim true and provable?
- Would the reader say these words out loud?

## Questions to ask
- What does the reader do today instead of buying this, and why is that worse?
- Which objection most often stops a sale?

## Pitfalls
- Writing for everyone ends up persuading no one; pick one reader.
- Fake urgency ("only 2 left" when untrue), fake reviews and hidden conditions are misleading and may break consumer law.
- Platform ad rules limit claims (health, finance, before/after); check the platform's current ad policy.
- Jargon the team uses internally rarely matches how customers describe the problem.

## Output
The core message sentence, 3 headline options, the full copy in the chosen structure, and a list of claims that need proof or softening.
