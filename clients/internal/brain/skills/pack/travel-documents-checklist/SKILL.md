# Travel documents checklist

Entry rules change and depend on nationality, destination, transit countries and purpose. Give a structured checklist, and always send the user to official sources for the final answer.

## Steps
1. **Collect the facts for each traveller.** Nationality (all passports held), residence permits, age, destination and every transit country, purpose (tourism, work, study), length of stay, dates, and transport (flight, car, cruise).
2. **Check identity documents.**
   - Is a passport needed, or is a national ID card accepted (for example, within much of the EU/EEA for EU citizens)?
   - Validity: many countries require the passport to be valid for 3–6 months beyond the stay and to have blank pages. Note the expiry date of each document.
   - Name on tickets must match the document exactly.
3. **Check entry permission.** Visa, visa on arrival, electronic travel authorisation (such as ESTA for the USA, eTA for Canada, ETA for the UK, ETIAS for the Schengen area once in force), or visa-free. Tell the user to confirm on the destination government's official site or embassy, and to beware of look-alike sites charging extra fees.
4. **Check transit rules.** Some airports require a transit visa even without leaving the airport.
5. **Children.** Each child needs their own document. A child travelling with one parent or with other adults may need a notarised consent letter from the other parent(s); check the destination and departure country rules.
6. **Driving.** Driving licence, an International Driving Permit if required, vehicle registration, insurance valid abroad (green card where applicable), road vignettes or toll tags.
7. **Health and insurance.** Travel insurance covering medical care and repatriation; for EU citizens travelling in the EU, the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) in addition, not instead. Required vaccinations or health forms, and rules for carrying medicines (some are restricted; carry a prescription or doctor's letter).
8. **Pets.** Pet passport, microchip, vaccinations and timing rules; check the official rules early.
9. **Make copies.** Photos or scans stored offline and in a secure place, paper copies kept separately from the originals, emergency contacts and the embassy details.

## Pitfalls
- Relying on a forum or an old answer; rules change, confirm on official sources.
- Paying third-party sites for visas or authorisations at inflated prices; use the official portal (type the address yourself, do not click links from ads or emails).
- Expired or nearly expired documents found at the airport; check months ahead, as renewals take time.
- Sharing full document numbers in chat; keep only the last digits in the checklist.
- Assuming a residence permit gives visa-free travel elsewhere.

## Output
Hand back: a per-traveller checklist with status and deadlines, the specific items to confirm and on which kind of official source (destination government, embassy, the user's foreign ministry travel advice), and a reminder list of copies to make.
