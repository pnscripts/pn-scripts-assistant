# Photo composition

Composition decides where the eye goes. Start with one clear subject, then remove everything that competes with it.

## Steps
1. **Name the subject in one sentence.** If the user cannot ("a nice street"), the photo has no subject yet; pick one person, object or shape.
2. **Check the edges first.** Walk around the frame: cut-off feet or hands at joints, half objects poking in, bright spots, signs, bins, poles growing out of heads. Fix by stepping, not only by cropping.
3. **Place the subject.**
   - Rule of thirds: subject or horizon on a third line; eyes on the top third in portraits.
   - Centre for symmetry, reflections, doors, tunnels and strong head-on portraits.
   - Leave space in front of a face or moving subject (lead room).
4. **Use lines and layers.** Roads, rails, fences and shorelines leading towards the subject; foreground, middle and background for depth; frames such as windows, arches or branches.
5. **Simplify the background.** Move sideways, shoot from lower or higher, use a wider aperture or a longer focal length, or come closer. "Fill the frame" fixes most snapshots.
6. **Choose the viewpoint.** Eye level for people, child height for children and pets, low angle to make things grand, high angle to show patterns and layouts.
7. **Read the light.** Side light shows texture and shape; backlight gives rims and silhouettes; flat midday light suits shade or overcast scenes. Golden hour is the hour after sunrise and before sunset.
8. **Straighten and crop.** Level horizons and verticals on buildings; crop to the destination's ratio (3:2 and 4:3 for print, 4:5 for portrait feeds, 9:16 for stories and reels, 16:9 for video and screens).

## Critique template
- Subject: what it is, does it read in one second?
- Distractions: list them with their location ("bright sign, top left").
- Balance and placement: what to move, and in which direction.
- Light: direction and quality, and a better time or position.
- Fix now (crop, straighten, darken edges) vs fix next time (step left, kneel, wait for the person to pass).

## Example
Photo: friend in front of a fountain, centred, small in the frame, pole behind the head.
Advice: take two steps closer and one step right so the pole leaves the head; put the friend on the left third with the fountain on the right; lower the camera to chest height; for the existing shot, crop to 4:5 and remove the pole with a healing tool.

## Pitfalls
- Rules are tools; breaking the thirds with a purpose (symmetry, tension) is fine, accidental tilt is not.
- Wide-angle lenses close to faces enlarge noses; use 50–85 mm equivalent for portraits.
- Heavy cropping costs resolution; check the final size is enough for print (about 300 pixels per inch at the print size).
- Do not over-advise: give the two or three changes that matter most.

## Output
Hand back a short critique (subject, top distractions, placement, light), the best crop with its ratio, and two or three concrete changes for the next shot.
