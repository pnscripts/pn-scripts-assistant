# Nutrition basics

Offer practical, general information for healthy adults. This is not medical advice: no diagnosis, no therapeutic diets, no supplement or medication doses. Refer to a doctor or a registered dietitian for any medical condition, pregnancy, eating disorder concerns, children's growth, or rapid weight change.

## When to refer to a doctor first
Diabetes, kidney, liver, heart or digestive disease, food allergies, pregnancy or breastfeeding, taking medication that interacts with food, a history of disordered eating, unexplained weight loss, or plans for very low-calorie diets or fasting. Urgent care (112 in the EU) for signs of a severe allergic reaction: swelling of lips or throat, difficulty breathing, fainting.

## Steps
1. **Understand the goal and context:** what the user wants (more energy, weight management, cheaper meals, more vegetables, sports), current eating pattern, budget, cooking time, culture and preferences, restrictions.
2. **Use a simple plate model:** about half vegetables and fruit, a quarter whole grains or starchy foods, a quarter protein (legumes, fish, eggs, dairy, poultry, meat), plus some healthy fats and water. Tell the user to check their national dietary guidelines for current specifics.
3. **Pick 2-3 small changes** rather than a full overhaul (e.g. vegetables at lunch and dinner, water instead of sugary drinks, a planned snack).
4. **Plan meals:** a week of simple dinners with leftovers for lunch, built from a few repeated ingredients to save money and time.
5. **Make the shopping list** grouped by store section; prefer frozen vegetables, legumes, oats, eggs and seasonal produce for budget.
6. **Read labels:** compare per 100 g, not per portion; look at sugars, salt, saturated fat and fibre; the ingredient list is in order of quantity.
7. **Review** after two weeks: what was easy, what was hard, adjust.

## Example: label comparison
| Per 100 g | Cereal A | Cereal B |
|---|---|---|
| Sugars | 24 g | 6 g |
| Fibre | 3 g | 9 g |
| Salt | 0.9 g | 0.2 g |
> Cereal B has a quarter of the sugar and three times the fibre. Add fruit for sweetness.

## Example: one balanced day
Breakfast: oats with yoghurt and berries. Lunch: leftover bean and vegetable stew with bread. Snack: apple and a handful of nuts. Dinner: baked fish or tofu, potatoes, salad. Water through the day.

## Pitfalls
- Recommending supplements or doses; send that question to a doctor or pharmacist.
- Moralising food as "good" or "bad"; focus on patterns.
- Extreme restriction, which rarely lasts and can be harmful.
- Health claims from adverts or influencers presented as facts; check official sources.
- Ignoring the user's budget, culture and cooking skills.

## Shopping list template (one week, budget-friendly)
- Vegetables: frozen mixed vegetables, onions, carrots, cabbage, tomatoes, seasonal greens
- Fruit: apples, bananas, frozen berries
- Grains: oats, wholegrain bread, rice, pasta
- Protein: dried or canned beans and lentils, eggs, yoghurt, cheese, canned fish, chicken
- Extras: olive or sunflower oil, nuts, spices, herbs

## Output
Hand back: 2-3 concrete changes, a weekly meal plan and shopping list if asked, label explanations, any reason to consult a doctor or dietitian, and a reminder that this is general information.
