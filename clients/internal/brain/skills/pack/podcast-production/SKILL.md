# Podcast production

Help the user ship consistent, listenable episodes with the least friction. Hosting platforms and directory rules change: tell the user to check the current requirements of their host and each directory.

## Steps
1. **Shape the show.** Topic and audience in one sentence, format (solo, co-hosts, interview, narrative), episode length, release rhythm (weekly, biweekly) and a working name. Check the name is not taken in podcast apps and as a domain.
2. **Kit.** One dynamic USB or XLR microphone per speaker is enough for most rooms; closed headphones for everyone; a quiet, soft room. Record each person on a separate track.
3. **Remote guests.** Use a tool that records each participant locally on their own device and uploads the file; as a backup, ask guests to record themselves on their phone. Send guests a short tech checklist in advance.
4. **Prepare the episode.** Outline, not a script: hook, intro, 3-5 segments with key questions, call to action. For interviews, send the guest the topics and agree on what is off limits.
5. **Record.** 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV; peaks around -12 dBFS; clap or count at the start for sync; 10 seconds of room silence.
6. **Edit.** Remove false starts, long pauses and filler that distracts (not every "um"). Keep the conversation natural. Add intro and outro music only if licensed.
7. **Mix and master.** Clean each voice (see audio cleanup), level speakers against each other, compress lightly, loudness around -16 LUFS stereo with true peak at or below -1 dBTP (check the host's current recommendation). Export MP3 for distribution (e.g. 128 kbps mono or 192 kbps stereo is common) and keep a WAV master.
8. **Publish.** Upload to a podcast host, which creates the RSS feed. Fill in title, episode number, description, show notes with timestamps and links, explicit flag, and the episode artwork if used.
9. **Distribute.** Submit the RSS feed once to the major directories through their creator portals; new episodes then appear automatically.
10. **Repurpose.** Short clips for social media, a transcript for accessibility and search, a newsletter or blog post.

## Show notes template
```
Episode 12 - <Title that states the benefit>
<2-3 sentence summary: who, what, why it matters>

In this episode:
00:00 Intro
02:15 <Topic 1>
14:40 <Topic 2>
31:05 <Takeaways>

Links mentioned: <list>
Guest: <name, role, where to follow>
Feedback: name@example.com
```

## Pitfalls
- Recording everyone on one microphone or one mixed track makes editing and levelling hard.
- Unlicensed music or clips can get episodes removed from directories.
- Cover art must meet each directory's current size and format rules (it is typically a large square image); check before upload.
- Inconsistent release days lose listeners faster than average audio quality.
- Changing the RSS feed address later without a proper redirect loses subscribers.

## Output
Return a plan that fits the user's stage: show concept and checklist for a new podcast, or an episode outline, recording settings, editing notes and filled-in show notes for a single episode.
