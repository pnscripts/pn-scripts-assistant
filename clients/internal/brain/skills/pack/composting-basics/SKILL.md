# Composting basics

Compost needs four things: a mix of nitrogen-rich "greens" and carbon-rich "browns", air, moisture like a wrung-out sponge, and time.

## Steps
1. **Collect the facts.** Space (garden, small yard, balcony, indoors), amount and type of waste (kitchen scraps, grass, leaves), time and effort available, local rules (some municipalities collect food waste or provide bins), and pests in the area.
2. **Choose a method.**
   - Open heap or bay: large gardens, cheap, slower unless turned.
   - Closed bin on soil: tidy, suits most gardens; takes 6–12 months.
   - Tumbler: easier turning, faster, smaller batches.
   - Worm bin (vermicomposting): flats or small spaces, kitchen scraps, kept out of frost and heat.
   - Bokashi: fermenting all kitchen waste in a sealed bucket indoors; the result must then be buried or added to a compost bin to finish.
3. **Site it.** Level, well-drained spot, partly shaded, easy to reach from the kitchen; bins on bare soil let worms in.
4. **Balance greens and browns.** Roughly 2–3 parts browns to 1 part greens by volume. Add in layers or mix each addition.
5. **Manage moisture and air.** Squeeze test: a few drops of water, not a stream. Turn or poke holes every 2–4 weeks for faster results.
6. **Chop and keep adding.** Smaller pieces break down faster.
7. **Harvest.** Ready when dark, crumbly, smells earthy and the original materials are unrecognisable; sieve out lumps and return them to the bin.
8. **Use it.** Spread 2–5 cm on beds, mix into planting holes, or blend with potting mix (about a quarter to a third compost).

## What goes in
| Greens (nitrogen) | Browns (carbon) | Keep out |
|---|---|---|
| Fruit and vegetable scraps | Dry leaves, straw | Meat, fish, bones, dairy (attract pests; bokashi can handle them) |
| Coffee grounds, tea leaves (plastic-free bags) | Shredded cardboard and plain paper | Cooked food with oil (small amounts in bokashi only) |
| Fresh grass cuttings (thin layers) | Twigs, wood chips | Diseased plants, weeds with seeds or running roots |
| Plant trimmings | Egg cartons, paper towels without chemicals | Pet waste, cat litter, ash from coal, glossy or coated paper |

## Fixing problems
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rotten or ammonia smell | Too wet or too many greens | Add browns, turn to add air |
| Nothing happens | Too dry, too many browns, too cold | Add greens and water, turn, insulate |
| Flies | Exposed food scraps | Bury scraps under browns, close the lid |
| Rats or mice | Cooked food, open base | Remove food waste types that attract them, use a closed bin with a mesh base |

## Pitfalls
- Only grass clippings; they turn into a slimy, smelly mat.
- Adding big woody pieces and expecting fast results.
- Using "compostable" plastics in home bins; many need industrial facilities, check the label.
- Using unfinished compost around seedlings; it can harm them.
- Ignoring local rules on food waste or bins; check with the municipality.
