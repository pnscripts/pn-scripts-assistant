# Home maintenance checklist

Regular small checks prevent expensive failures. Separate what an owner can safely do from work that needs a qualified professional.

## Steps
1. **Collect the facts.** House or flat, age, owned or rented, heating type (gas boiler, heat pump, electric, district heating, wood or pellet stove), water heater, climate and season, garden or balcony, and known problems.
2. **Mark responsibilities.** Tenants usually report faults to the landlord; flat owners share roof, risers and facade with the building association. Tell the user to check their lease or building rules.
3. **Build the calendar** from the lists below, removing items that do not apply.
4. **Flag professional work.** Gas appliances, electrical wiring, roof work, structural cracks and chimney sweeping must be done by qualified people; local rules may require certified installers.
5. **Create a log**: date, task, who did it, cost, next due date, receipts and warranty documents.

## Seasonal checklist
**Autumn (before the heating season)**
- Boiler or heat pump service by a qualified technician; bleed radiators that are cold at the top.
- Chimney and flue sweep if you burn wood, pellets or coal.
- Clear gutters and downpipes; check roof for missing tiles from the ground (binoculars, not a ladder on a wet roof).
- Seal draughts around windows and doors; check outdoor taps and insulate exposed pipes.
**Winter**
- Know where the main water stop valve is and test that it turns.
- Keep a minimum heating level in unused rooms to avoid frozen pipes and condensation.
- Watch for condensation and mould on cold walls; ventilate briefly but often.
**Spring**
- Check for winter damage: roof, facade, gutters, fences.
- Clean or replace air-conditioner filters; have the system checked before summer.
- Test outdoor drainage after heavy rain.
**Summer**
- Repaint or treat exterior wood; check window seals.
- Clean the extractor hood filter, fridge door seals and coils (where accessible).

## Monthly and yearly
- Monthly: test smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms; test the residual-current device (RCD) with its test button; check under sinks for leaks; clean the dishwasher and washing machine filters.
- Every 6–12 months: descale the water heater (or have it serviced), check the anode if applicable, check silicone seals in the bathroom and kitchen, check fire extinguisher pressure.
- Replace alarm batteries yearly or as stated; replace alarms at the end of their marked lifespan.

## Call a professional when
- You smell gas: do not use switches or flames, open windows, leave, and call the gas emergency number from outside (in the EU 112 if in doubt).
- A fuse or RCD trips repeatedly, sockets are warm or scorched, or lights flicker for no reason.
- Water stains spread, the water meter turns with all taps closed, or cracks widen.

## Pitfalls
- Working on a ladder alone or on a wet roof.
- DIY gas or electrical repairs beyond changing a bulb or resetting a breaker.
- Painting over damp patches without finding the cause.
- Forgetting receipts and warranty dates.

## Output
Hand back: a month-by-month or season-by-season checklist tailored to the home, tasks marked "owner" or "professional", urgent warning signs, and a simple log template.
