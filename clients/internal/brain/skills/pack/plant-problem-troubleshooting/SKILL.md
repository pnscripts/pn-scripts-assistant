# Plant problem troubleshooting

Most plant problems come from growing conditions (water, light, temperature, soil), not disease. Check the basics first, then pests, then disease.

## Steps
1. **Collect the facts.** Plant name (or a photo), indoor or outdoor, age, where it grows (pot, bed, sun hours), watering habit, recent changes (repotting, moving, weather, fertiliser), which leaves are affected (old/lower or new/upper), pattern of symptoms, and how fast it spread.
2. **Check water first.** Push a finger 3–5 cm into the soil. Wet and smelly soil, yellow lower leaves and wilting despite moisture point to overwatering or poor drainage. Dry, crispy edges and drooping that recovers after watering point to underwatering.
3. **Check light and temperature.** Leggy pale growth: too little light. Bleached or scorched patches on the sunny side: sunburn. Sudden leaf drop: cold draught, heat or a recent move.
4. **Check soil and nutrients.** Old leaves yellow evenly: often nitrogen shortage. Yellow between veins on new leaves: often iron shortage or alkaline soil (high pH). Blossom-end rot on tomatoes or peppers: usually uneven watering affecting calcium uptake. White crust on pots: salt build-up from fertiliser or hard water.
5. **Look for pests.** Check under leaves and at growing tips with a magnifier: aphids (clusters of small soft insects), spider mites (fine webbing, speckled leaves), whitefly (tiny white flies when disturbed), scale (brown bumps), slugs and snails (irregular holes, slime trails, mostly at night), caterpillars (holes and droppings).
6. **Look for disease.** Powdery white coating (powdery mildew), brown spots with yellow halos (leaf spot), grey fuzzy mould (botrytis), sudden wilting of a healthy-looking plant (root or wilt disease), lower leaves of tomatoes with dark rings (early blight).
7. **Treat in steps, gentlest first.** Fix conditions; remove affected leaves; hand-pick or wash off pests with water; improve air flow; then targeted products only if needed, following the label exactly.
8. **Prevent recurrence.** Right plant for the place, good spacing, watering at the base, crop rotation, clean tools, inspect new plants before bringing them home.

## Quick decision table
| Symptom | Most likely | First action |
|---|---|---|
| Lower leaves yellow, soil wet | Overwatering | Let dry, improve drainage, water less often |
| Leaf edges brown and crispy | Underwatering or dry air | Water deeply, check routine and humidity |
| Holes in leaves, slime trails | Slugs/snails | Collect at dusk, barriers, keep area tidy |
| Sticky leaves, small insects | Aphids | Spray off with water, encourage ladybirds |
| White powder on leaves | Powdery mildew | Remove worst leaves, better air flow, water soil not leaves |
| No fruit on tomatoes/courgettes | Poor pollination or heat | Gently shake flowers or hand-pollinate; wait out heat |

## Questions to ask before answering
- Did it start suddenly or slowly, and after what change?
- Are new leaves healthy? If yes, the cause may already be fixed.
- Are neighbouring plants of the same kind affected too?
- Has any product been sprayed nearby recently (including weedkiller drift)?

## When to get local expert help
A valuable tree, a fast-spreading problem across many plants, or a suspected regulated pest or disease: suggest a local nursery, the agricultural advisory service or a qualified arborist.

## Pitfalls
- Spraying products before checking water and light.
- Treating with a pesticide for a problem that is not a pest.
- Composting diseased plant material in a cool compost heap; bin it instead if disease is suspected.
- Using chemicals on edible crops without checking the label's harvest interval.
- Advising that a plant is safe to eat or safe for pets without being sure; some plants are toxic to children, cats and dogs.

## Output
Hand back: the most likely cause (and alternatives if uncertain), what to check to confirm, a step-by-step treatment from gentlest to strongest, prevention tips, and what improvement to expect and when.
