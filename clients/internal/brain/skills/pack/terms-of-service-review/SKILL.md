# Terms of service review

Turn long terms into a short, honest summary of the user's obligations and risks. This is not legal advice; when money at stake is significant, a dispute has started, or the terms govern the user's business income, recommend a lawyer.

## Steps
1. **Get the right text.** The full current terms from the official site, with date or version, and any documents they incorporate (pricing page, acceptable use policy, seller agreement). Do not follow links from unsolicited messages.
2. **Ask the user's goal:** sign up, buy, sell or publish on a platform, cancel, or dispute something. Focus the review on that.
3. **Extract the key clauses** into a table with section numbers:
   - Price, billing cycle, auto-renewal, price changes, refunds
   - Cancellation: how, notice period, what happens to data
   - Licence to the service: what you may and may not do
   - Your content: who owns it, what licence you grant, can they use it for training or ads
   - Termination or suspension by the provider, and on what grounds
   - Liability limits, warranties, indemnities you give
   - Governing law, jurisdiction, arbitration, class-action waiver
   - Changes to the terms and how you are told
4. **Flag clauses** as normal, worth noting, or unusual and one-sided, quoting the short sentence.
5. **Note consumer protections** that may apply regardless of the terms (in the EU, e.g. withdrawal rights for many online purchases and rules against unfair terms) as general orientation, and suggest checking with a consumer protection body for a specific case.
6. **Give actions:** settings to change, dates to diarise, screenshots to keep, questions to send to support.

## Example summary
> You pay monthly; the plan renews automatically and the price can rise with 30 days' notice (s. 4.2). You can cancel any time in Account settings; access ends at the end of the paid month, no partial refunds (s. 4.5). You keep ownership of your uploads but give them a worldwide licence to host and display them, which ends when you delete them (s. 7.1). Unusual: they may suspend your account "for any reason" without notice (s. 9.3). Keep your own backups.

## Pitfalls
- Reviewing an outdated copy; check the date.
- Missing the incorporated documents where fees and rules really live.
- Treating "we may" clauses as harmless; they define what the provider is allowed to do.
- Assuming a clause is enforceable just because it is written; some are not under consumer law, but only a lawyer can say for a specific case.

## Quick checklist before accepting
- [ ] I know the total price per year, including renewals
- [ ] I know how and by when to cancel
- [ ] I keep ownership of my content, and I understand the licence I grant
- [ ] I know when they can close my account and what happens to my data
- [ ] I have a copy or screenshot of the terms and the date I accepted

## Template: question to support
```
Hello, before I subscribe I would like to confirm two points in your terms
(version dated ...): section 4.5 on refunds and section 9.3 on account suspension.
Could you explain how they apply to a monthly plan? Thank you.
```

## Output
Hand back: a plain-language summary (5-10 lines), the key-clause table with section numbers, flagged clauses with short quotes, practical actions, and the statement that this is not legal advice and when to see a lawyer.
