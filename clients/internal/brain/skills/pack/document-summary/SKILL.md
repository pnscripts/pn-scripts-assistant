# Document summary

A summary must be shorter, faithful and useful for a specific reader. Never add facts that are not in the document, and never drop a point that changes the meaning (a deadline, an exception, a cost).

## Steps
1. **Clarify reader and length.** Who will read it and why (decide, act, study, forward)? Default: 5-7 bullet points plus a one-sentence gist. Do not ask if the context already makes it clear.
2. **Skim the structure.** Read title, contents, headings, abstract or introduction, conclusion, tables and figures. Note the document type and date.
3. **Read for substance.** Mark the main claim or purpose, supporting points, numbers, dates, names, decisions, obligations, risks and exceptions.
4. **Rank.** Keep what the reader needs; drop examples, repetition and background unless asked.
5. **Write.**
   - Start with the gist in one sentence.
   - Then key points in order of importance, not page order.
   - Keep exact figures, dates and defined terms as written.
   - Use neutral wording; attribute opinions ("the author argues").
6. **Add actions and open points** if the document asks something of the reader.
7. **Check against the source** with the checklist before handing back.

## Length guide
- One line: gist only.
- Short: gist + 3-7 bullets (default).
- Detailed: one paragraph per section, with section references.

## Template
```
Gist: <one sentence>
Key points
- ...
Numbers and dates: ...
What you need to do: ... (by <date>)
Not covered / unclear: ...
Source: <title, author, date, pages or sections>
```

## Example
A 30-page supplier contract summarised for a manager: gist (2-year supply of office equipment, auto-renews), key points (price fixed for year one, then indexed; 60-day notice to end; penalties for late delivery capped at 10%), action (decide on renewal 60 days before the end date), unclear (who pays return shipping).

## Checklist
- [ ] Every number, date and name matches the source
- [ ] No new facts or opinions added
- [ ] Exceptions, conditions and "unless" clauses kept
- [ ] The author's view is separated from established fact
- [ ] Length matches the request

## Pitfalls
- Summarising only the first pages of a long file; cover the whole document or say which part you covered.
- Softening strong wording ("must" becomes "should") or hardening weak wording.
- For contracts, policies and medical or legal documents, add that the summary does not replace reading the original and expert advice where it matters.
- Scanned PDFs may have text recognition errors in numbers; flag doubtful figures.

## Output
Hand back the summary in the template at the requested length, with section or page references for key points and a note of anything unclear or not covered.
