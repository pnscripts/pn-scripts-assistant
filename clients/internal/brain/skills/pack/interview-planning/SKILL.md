# Interview planning

Structured interviews predict job performance far better than free conversation. Same questions, same scale, notes taken during the interview.

## Steps
1. **Start from the job description.** Pick 4-6 competencies that truly decide success (e.g. problem solving, customer focus, a specific technical skill, written communication). Drop anything you cannot assess in the time.
2. **Plan the stages.** Typical flow: 20-30 min screening call -> 45-60 min structured interview -> optional short work sample -> final conversation. Keep the total effort for the candidate reasonable and say up front how long each stage takes.
3. **Write 2 questions per competency.**
   - Behavioural (past): "Tell me about a time you..."
   - Situational (future): "What would you do if..."
   Add one follow-up for each ("What was your part exactly?", "What happened in the end?").
4. **Define what good looks like.** For each question, write a 1-3-5 anchor: what a weak, adequate and strong answer contains. This is the scoring guide.
5. **Design the work sample** (optional). A task of at most 1-2 hours that mirrors the real job, the same for everyone, unpaid work never used commercially. Say how it will be judged.
6. **Assign the panel.** Each interviewer owns certain competencies; one person keeps time and explains the next steps. Brief the panel on the questions to avoid (below).
7. **Prepare the opening and close.** Opening: introductions, the agenda, "I will take notes". Close: candidate questions, next steps, timeline.
8. **Plan adjustments.** Ask every candidate in advance whether they need any adjustment (accessibility, time, format).

## Questions never to ask
Do not ask about age, date of birth, marital status, children or plans for them, pregnancy, religion, ethnicity, nationality beyond the legal right to work, sexual orientation, health, disability, union membership or political views. If a candidate volunteers such information, do not note it or use it. Rules differ by country; check local labour and equal-treatment law or ask an HR or legal professional.

## Checklist
- [ ] Competencies come from the job, not from "culture fit" gut feeling
- [ ] Same core questions and order for every candidate
- [ ] Scoring anchors written before the first interview
- [ ] Each interviewer scores alone before discussing
- [ ] Notes record evidence (what was said), not impressions
- [ ] Candidate told the stages, duration and when they will hear back

## Example question with scoring guide
Competency: customer focus.
Question: "Tell me about a time a customer was angry with something that was not your fault. What did you do?"
- 1: blames the customer or the company, no action described.
- 3: calmed the customer and solved the immediate problem.
- 5: solved it, followed up, and changed something so it would not happen again.

## Interview plan template
```
Role: [title]   Date: [ ]   Candidate: [ID]   Panel: [names/roles]
0-5 min    Welcome, agenda, notes
5-40 min   Competency questions (Q1-Q8) - interviewer A/B
40-50 min  Work sample discussion
50-60 min  Candidate questions, next steps
Scores: [competency] 1/3/5 + evidence
```

## Pitfalls
- Trick or brainteaser questions; they predict little and put people off.
- Talking more than the candidate; aim for the candidate speaking 70-80% of the time.
- Judging on the first five minutes, likeability or similarity to yourself.
- Changing questions between candidates, which makes comparison unfair.

## Output
Hand back the interview plan: competencies, stages with timing, the question set with follow-ups and 1-3-5 anchors, the work sample brief if any, and a one-page scoring sheet.
