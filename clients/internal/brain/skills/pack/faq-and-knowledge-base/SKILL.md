# FAQ and knowledge base

A good help article answers one question, in the customer's words, and gets them to success in the fewest steps.

## Steps
1. **Collect real questions.** Use support tickets, chat logs, emails, reviews, sales calls and search terms. Count how often each comes up; start with the top 10-20.
2. **Group and deduplicate.** Merge questions that have the same answer. Group into 4-8 categories the customer understands (Orders, Payment, Account, Using the product, Troubleshooting), not internal team names.
3. **Pick the format.**
   - Short FAQ entry (2-5 sentences) for simple facts: delivery times, payment methods, opening hours.
   - How-to article with numbered steps for tasks.
   - Troubleshooting article: symptom → cause → fix, from the most common cause to the rarest.
4. **Write each answer** (template below): title as the user's question, direct answer in the first sentence, then details.
5. **Verify accuracy.** Every fact, price, menu path and policy must be confirmed by the user or the current product. Mark anything that changes often and add a "last reviewed" date.
6. **Make it findable.** Use the words customers use ("cancel my order", not "order termination"), add synonyms, link related articles, keep URLs short.
7. **Plan maintenance.** Assign an owner, review quarterly and after every product or policy change, and track which articles do not solve the problem (repeat tickets after reading).

## Article template
**Title:** How do I change my delivery address?

**Answer:** You can change the address until the order is shipped, from *My orders → Order details → Edit address*.

**Steps:**
1. Sign in and open *My orders*.
2. Choose the order and click *Edit address*.
3. Enter the new address and click *Save*. You will get a confirmation email.

**If the order is already shipped:** contact the courier with the tracking number, or write to support@example.com.

**Related:** Delivery times · Tracking my order
*Last reviewed: 26.10.2026*

## Checklist
- One question per article; title phrased as the customer would ask it.
- The answer is in the first sentence; no marketing introduction.
- Steps are numbered, one action each, with exact button and menu names.
- No internal jargon, no promises the policy does not support.
- Legal and policy texts (returns, privacy, warranty) link to the official policy page instead of paraphrasing it loosely.
- Each article has a review date and an owner.

## Pitfalls
- Writing FAQs the company wishes customers asked ("Why is our product the best?").
- Long pages with dozens of questions and no categories or search.
- Screenshots and menu paths that are outdated after an update.
- Answers that end in "contact support" without trying to solve the issue.
- Duplicate articles giving different answers to the same question.

## Output
Return the category structure, the list of questions ranked by frequency, the finished entries or articles in the template format, and a list of facts the user must confirm before publishing.
