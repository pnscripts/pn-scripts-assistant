# CV writing

A CV is a marketing document for one job, not a life history. Every line should help the reader decide "this person can do our job".

## Steps
1. **Collect material:** current CV or work history, the target job ad, country (conventions differ: photo, date of birth and length vary; Europass is common in parts of the EU), and the user's top achievements.
2. **Read the job ad** and list the 5-8 most important requirements and keywords. The CV must show evidence for each.
3. **Choose the format:** reverse chronological for most people; skills-first (combination) for career changers or gaps. One column, standard headings, a simple font, exported as PDF unless the employer asks for Word.
4. **Write the profile** (3-4 lines): who you are professionally, years and field, 2-3 strengths that match the ad, what you want to do next.
5. **Rewrite experience as achievements.** Formula: action verb + what you did + result (number, scale or effect). 3-6 bullets per recent role, fewer for older roles.
6. **Skills section:** hard skills and tools matching the ad, languages with level (e.g. CEFR B2), certifications. Avoid long lists of generic soft skills; prove them in bullets instead.
7. **Education and extras:** degree, relevant courses, volunteering, projects or portfolio link.
8. **Length:** usually 1 page for early career, up to 2 pages for experienced people; academic CVs can be longer.
9. **Proofread and check:** consistent dates and tense, no typos, contact details current, file name `Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf`.

## Bullet examples
- Weak: "Responsible for social media."
- Better: "Grew Instagram following from 2,000 to 11,000 in 12 months by launching a weekly video series and partnering with 6 local creators."
- Weak: "Handled customer complaints."
- Better: "Resolved 30+ customer cases a day and cut the average response time from 24 to 6 hours by building a reply template library."

## ATS-friendly checklist
- Standard headings: Profile, Experience, Education, Skills.
- Keywords from the ad used naturally, spelled the same way.
- No text in images, tables or headers/footers for key info.
- Dates as "Mar 2021 - Present" consistently.

## Questions to ask
- Which 3 achievements are you most proud of, and what changed because of them?
- What numbers can we use (money, time, volume, people, ratings)?
- Are there gaps or a career change that the profile should explain briefly?
- Which roles are you applying for next, so the profile can be adapted?

## Pitfalls
- One generic CV for every application.
- Duties instead of results; adjectives ("dynamic, hard-working") instead of proof.
- Inventing or inflating experience; it collapses in interviews and reference checks.
- Personal data the employer does not need (ID number, marital status, full address); check local norms.

## Output
The tailored CV in plain, copy-ready text with clear headings, a list of the job's key requirements mapped to CV evidence, and questions about any missing numbers or achievements.
