# Returns and refunds policy

A clear policy reduces disputes and builds trust. It must never promise less than the law gives consumers. This is general information, not legal advice; for edge cases or disputes, consult a lawyer or the national consumer protection authority.

## Steps
1. **Collect facts:** country of the shop, countries sold to, consumer or business buyers, product types (physical, personalised, perishable, digital content, services), current process and costs.
2. **Separate two different rights:**
   - **Change of mind (distance sales).** In the EU, consumers buying online generally have a 14-day right of withdrawal from delivery, without giving a reason, with legal exceptions (e.g. made-to-order or personalised items, perishable goods, sealed hygiene items once unsealed, digital content once download started with consent). Check the current rules for the user's country.
   - **Faulty or not as described.** A legal guarantee of conformity applies regardless of the shop's own policy (in the EU at least two years for goods; check national rules).
3. **Decide the shop's choices** within the law: longer window than the minimum, free or paid return shipping (the policy must say who pays), exchanges, store credit as an option (not a replacement for a refund where a refund is due).
4. **Draft the policy** in plain language with the sections below.
5. **Write the internal process:** who approves, how goods are inspected, refund deadline (in the EU generally within 14 days of the withdrawal notice, may be held until goods are received or proof of sending), how refunds are made (same payment method unless agreed otherwise).

## Policy template
1. Your right to return (window, how it is counted, no reason needed).
2. How to start a return (form or email name@example.com, what to include).
3. Condition of items (tried as in a shop is fine; reduced value for excessive handling may be deducted where allowed).
4. Items that cannot be returned (list the legal exceptions that apply).
5. Return shipping (who pays, address, tracking advice).
6. Refunds (what is refunded, including standard outbound delivery, method, timing).
7. Faulty or wrong items (repair, replacement or refund, no cost to the customer).
8. Exchanges and contact.

## Example
A customer asks to return a sweater 10 days after delivery because the colour looks different.
- Within the 14-day withdrawal period, no reason needed: accept.
- Customer pays return shipping only if the policy said so before purchase.
- Refund the price and the standard delivery cost within the legal deadline once the item or proof of sending arrives.

## Checklist
- The policy is linked from the footer, product pages and order confirmation.
- The withdrawal information and model form are given before purchase.
- Exceptions listed are real legal exceptions for the products sold.

## Pitfalls
- "No refunds" or "store credit only" wording is invalid for consumers in many countries.
- Charging restocking fees or refusing returns of opened packaging without a legal basis.
- Forgetting to give consumers the withdrawal information and model form before purchase; this can extend the return window.
- Rules differ by country and change; verify against the official consumer authority's current guidance.

## Output
The customer-facing policy text, a short internal process checklist, and a list of points the user should confirm with a lawyer or consumer authority.
