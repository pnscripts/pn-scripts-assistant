# Product photography

Buyers cannot touch the product, so the photos must answer their questions: what it is, how big, what it is made of, what is included, and how it looks in use.

## Kit (cheap is fine)
- Phone or camera on a tripod or stack of books; use the 2-second timer.
- Light: a large window with indirect daylight, or two daylight LED lamps of the same colour temperature.
- White sweep: a sheet of A2/A1 paper or card curved from wall to table, no corner line.
- White foam board or card as a reflector; black card to add contrast to glass or metal.
- Tape, putty and clamps to hold items in place.

## Steps
1. **Check the platform rules first.** Each marketplace sets its own: background colour, minimum size, how much of the frame the product fills, banned text, watermarks and borders. Read the current image requirements on the platform's help page.
2. **Prepare the product.** Clean dust and fingerprints (gloves for metal and glass), steam clothes, remove tags and stickers unless they matter.
3. **Set up the light.** Window or main lamp to one side, slightly behind the product; reflector on the other side to open shadows. Switch off ceiling lights to avoid mixed colours.
4. **Set the camera.** Lowest ISO, f/8–f/11 on a camera for front-to-back sharpness, white balance set on the white paper (or custom), focus on the front of the product. On a phone use 2× instead of the wide lens to avoid distortion.
5. **Shoot a standard set** for every product in the same order:
   1. Hero shot: front three-quarter view on white.
   2. Front, back, side, top as needed.
   3. Details: texture, stitching, label, connectors.
   4. Scale: in hand, next to a common object, or worn.
   5. What is in the box.
   6. Lifestyle: in use, in a real setting.
6. **Keep consistency.** Mark camera and product positions with tape; same distance, angle, light and crop for a whole range.
7. **Edit.** Crop to the platform's ratio (often square), set white balance, lift the background to pure white only if required, remove dust, keep colours true to the real item. Export sRGB JPEG at the platform's recommended size.

## Special cases
- **Jewellery and glass:** black and white cards to shape reflections; keep the camera out of shiny surfaces; clean with a lint-free cloth, not sprays.
- **Clothing:** flat lay from directly above, or on a mannequin or model; show the fit and a fabric close-up.
- **Food:** natural side light, shoot soon after preparation, small props, top-down for flat dishes, 45° for drinks and burgers.

## Pitfalls
- Colours that differ from the real product lead to returns and complaints; never "improve" the colour of the item itself.
- Mixed light (daylight + warm bulb) gives colour casts that are hard to fix.
- Wide phone lenses close up distort shapes; step back and zoom 2×.
- Photos of other brands' logos, copyrighted artwork or people need permission; use your own photos, not the manufacturer's, unless the licence allows it.
- Heavy shadows under the item on white backgrounds look dirty; lift the item on clear acrylic or add fill light.

## Output
Hand back a setup for the user's space and product, a numbered shot list, camera settings, editing and export settings matched to the target platform, and the rules to verify on that platform's current help page.
