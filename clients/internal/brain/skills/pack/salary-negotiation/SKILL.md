# Salary negotiation

Negotiation is a normal, polite part of hiring. Prepare numbers from evidence, talk about value, and stay friendly and specific. This is general guidance, not financial or legal advice; check employment contracts and local labour law for specifics.

## Steps
1. **Research the market range** for the role, level, city or remote region: salary surveys, job ads that publish pay, public sector scales, recruiters, people in similar roles. Note whether figures are gross or net, monthly or yearly.
2. **Set three numbers:** target (ambitious but defensible, around the upper part of the range for strong fit), acceptable, and walk-away (below this, decline). Write down why.
3. **Value the whole package:** base pay, bonus, equity, paid leave, remote work, training budget, health insurance, transport or food vouchers, notice period, start date, title. Decide what matters most.
4. **Handle "What are your expectations?"** early in the process: give a researched range whose bottom is still acceptable, or ask for the budget for the role first.
5. **When the offer arrives:** thank them, show enthusiasm, ask for it in writing, take a day or two to review.
6. **Counter once, clearly,** with reasons tied to value: experience, skills that match, market data, competing offer (only if real).
7. **If base pay cannot move,** ask for alternatives: signing bonus, earlier review date with agreed criteria, extra leave, training, remote days.
8. **Close:** confirm the final terms in writing before resigning from the current job.

## Script - counter-offer email
```
Thank you for the offer for the [role] position - I am excited about joining [company].
Based on my [X years] in [field], my experience with [key skill from the ad] and market data for similar roles in [city], I was expecting a base salary of [number].
Is there flexibility to reach that figure? I am confident I can deliver [specific value] in the first months.
```

## Asking for a raise
- Timing: after a success, before budget planning, at the review.
- Evidence: results with numbers, extra responsibilities, market range.
- Ask: a specific number or percentage; if "not now", ask what must be true and by when, and put it in writing.

## Pitfalls
- Naming a number without research, or a single number with no range.
- Accepting or rejecting on the spot under pressure.
- Inventing competing offers; it can backfire badly.
- Negotiating aggressively or by ultimatum; you will work with these people.
- Comparing gross with net or monthly with annual figures.

## Output
The researched range with sources, the target/acceptable/walk-away numbers, a package comparison table, and ready-to-use scripts (spoken and email) for the user's situation.
