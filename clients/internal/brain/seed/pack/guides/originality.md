# Originality and licences

Everything made for you — pictures drawn by code, synthesised sounds and
music, names, characters, logos, interfaces, texts, levels and game rules —
has to be your own. Being generated by a program is not a licence loophole:
a copy drawn or played by code is still a copy.

## Not allowed, even "made by code"
- Protected characters, or characters made to look like them.
- Logos, trademarks and trade dress (the recognisable look of a product or
  its packaging).
- Known melodies, jingles and sound logos, also played "by ear".
- The signature style of one specific work or artist, level layouts copied
  from a known game, and names of existing products or works.

## What it needs instead
- **Its own name**, checked against trademarks and existing products before
  it is used.
- **Its own meaningful core idea.** Write one sentence: "what only this one
  does". If the closest famous work differs only in theme or skin, it is not
  original yet.
- Music and sounds composed for this work; pictures and characters designed
  for it.

## How to check
1. Name the three closest existing works and say, for each, what is
   different in the rules, the look, the sound and the name.
2. Search the name in trademark registers (for example EUIPO TMview, WIPO
   Global Brand Database) and in the stores where it will be published.
3. Look at what players or readers said about similar earlier work:
   "looks like…" and "same as…" are the measure.
4. Change whatever is too close, then check again.

The assistant runs this check before it reports any game, logo, music, story
or other creative result as finished, and puts what it found in the report.
