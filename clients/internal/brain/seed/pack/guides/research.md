# Research with sources

Open **Research** in the menu, or ask: "research what changed in EU rules on
online returns, with sources", "look into whether intermittent fasting
helps blood pressure", "check this paragraph against its sources".

## A question looked into properly
Write the question and choose **Quick** (four sources) or **Thorough**
(eight). I decide what to search for — the question itself first, then the
parts that need their own search: figures, dates, the official source, the
opposing view — and read the best of what is found. Official bodies and
scholarly sources come first, news organisations and documentation next,
forums and social media last, and never more than two pages from one site,
because ten pages of one site are one opinion. A page that cannot be read is
kept with its search summary and marked as only partly read.

## A report whose claims say where they came from
The report always has the same shape: a short answer, what the sources say,
where they disagree, and what they do not settle. Every sentence that states
a fact ends with the number of its source, and the list of sources at the
end says what kind of place each one is, the date it gives, and when it was
read. Click a number to jump to its source; click a source to open it in
your browser. **Download (.md)** saves the whole report as a file.

## Every claim checked
A model writes as confidently when it is wrong as when it is right, so the
finished report is checked sentence by sentence against its own sources:

- a number pointing to a source that does not exist is removed;
- a sentence that states something and cites nothing is marked;
- a figure that is in none of the sources it cites is marked;
- a sentence that shares almost none of its words with its source is marked.

What is marked is then put to the language model a second time, as a fact
checker that sees only the passage of the source and must judge from it
alone. The **Claims check** section lists every marked sentence and why.
Read those in their source before relying on them.

## Checking your own text
**Check a text against its sources** takes a text that puts [1], [2] …
after its claims — an essay, an article, an answer from anywhere — and its
sources in the same order, as web addresses or files on this computer, and
says for every sentence whether its source supports it. Nothing is kept.

## What leaves this computer
Research reads the web: the search words go to a search engine (checked
first for names, addresses and other personal details, which are never
sent) and each page is requested from its own site. It is not available
while the privacy setting keeps this machine to itself. The reports are kept
in the assistant's knowledge base on this computer, sealed with everything
else; **Remove** deletes one. This is information to help you understand a
question, not professional advice.
