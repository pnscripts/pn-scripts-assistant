# Learning: lessons, practice questions and review

Open **Learning** in the menu, or ask: "teach me fractions for a ten-year-old",
"quiz me on the French Revolution", "what should I revise today?".

## A lesson at your level
Name any topic and choose a level — **Child (8–12)**, **Beginner**,
**Intermediate** or **Advanced** — and how long it should take. The lesson
always has the same shape, so it is easy to follow: the goal, what you need to
know first, small steps each with a worked example, the mistakes people make,
questions to check yourself (their answers stay folded until you open them),
and what to learn next. It is written in the language of the topic, or the
one you choose, and kept with the topic.

## Practice questions
**Practise** writes 3, 5 or 10 questions on the topic: choices, numbers and
short answers — the kinds that can be marked fairly. Questions arrive one at
a time and without their answers; you see the right answer only after you
have given yours. Answers are marked by rules wherever a rule can tell: a
choice by its letter or text, a number however you write it (12,5 or 12.5,
1/3 or 0.33), a short answer whatever its capitals or punctuation. When no
rule can tell — the right idea in other words — the language model judges it
against the expected answer and says why; when there is no model, you see
the expected answer and mark it yourself.

## Questions come back until you know them
A question you miss comes back a few minutes later. One you answer right
comes back tomorrow, then in three days, a week and a month; after that it
counts as learnt. The list **What you are learning** shows every topic with
its questions, how many are learnt, the share answered right and what is
due, and **Review** asks only the questions that are due.

## Honest about what it is
Lessons and questions are written by a language model, which can be wrong.
It is told never to invent dates, numbers, names or sources and to say when
it is not sure; for anything important — an exam, a medical, legal or money
question — check with a textbook or a teacher. For homework I explain and
give hints rather than doing it for you. What you learn is kept in the
assistant's knowledge base on this computer, sealed with everything else,
and **Remove** forgets a topic and its questions.
