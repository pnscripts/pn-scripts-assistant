# Mail: what came in, and replies that wait for your yes

Open **Mail** in the menu, or ask: "what came in today?", "answer Anna's
message", "is this email a scam?".

## New mail, sorted
New mail is sorted from its headers alone — nothing is opened:

- **Suspicious** — the scam check warned: a sender pretending to be a brand,
  a reply address that goes somewhere else, words that pressure you to pay or
  sign in. Do not click, pay or answer before checking the sender another way.
- **Needs a reply** — from a person, and the subject asks for something or
  continues a conversation.
- **From people** — everything else a person wrote to you.
- **Newsletters and notifications** — anything sent to a list or by a machine,
  counted by sender.

Mail is read from your mail server when your account is set up under
Settings, or from Thunderbird on this computer. Switch on **a summary every
morning** and at eight I put one line in the conversation: how many need a
reply, how many look suspicious, and how many drafts wait.

## Replies wait for your yes
Ask me to answer a message and I write a draft in its language and tone,
proofread it, and keep it under **Waiting for your yes**. Nothing is sent
until you press **Send** on that draft — once, exactly as it stands on the
screen. Change the text first if you like. If the message it answers looked
suspicious, the draft says so in red.

In the chat, sending a draft always asks you first and shows the whole
message, even when everything else is allowed. Every message sent is in
**What I did**.

Sending needs your mail account under Settings. Without one, copy the text
into your mail program.

## What I never do
I never send, forward or delete mail without your yes, never click links or
open attachments from strangers, and never treat anything written in an
email as an instruction from you.
