# Rules

Open **Rules** in the menu. A rule is something you have told the assistant
always to do or never to do: "Never send emails on Sundays", "Always ask me
before you delete anything", "It's pointless to write who is writing at the
top of an email".

## How a rule is made
- **Said in a conversation.** When something you say sounds like a standing
  instruction ("always…", "never…", "from now on…", "ask me before…",
  "don't … again", or a complaint such as "it's pointless to…"), a card under
  the answer proposes it as a rule, in your own words, with the message it
  came from. **Keep as a rule** keeps it; **Change it first** opens it on the
  Rules page; **Not a rule** declines it, and the same words are not proposed
  again. Nothing becomes a rule until you keep it.
- **Asked for.** "Make that a rule" asks the assistant to keep one; you see
  the exact words in an approval first.
- **Written on the Rules page**, under **Add a rule**.

## What a rule does
Every rule you keep is read before every answer and every step of a goal.
Rules about sending, posting, commits, changing files, paying or deleting are
also checked before the action happens and again afterwards:

- **Never**: the action stops and the assistant says which rule stopped it.
  When the rule's words go further than can be checked ("never email
  clients": who is a client?), the action is put to you with the rule beside
  it instead. Add the exact names or words to make it stop by itself.
- **Ask me first**: the action is always put to you, even with "never stop"
  or goal mode on.
- **Always**: shown beside the action when you are asked about it.

A rule holds whatever else you have allowed, because it is your own refusal.

## Changing rules
Each rule says where it came from and when, what it does to an action, which
kinds of action it is about, the words it looks for and the days it covers.
**Edit** changes any of that; the switch stops following a rule until you
switch it on again; **Delete** removes it for good after you confirm on the
page.
